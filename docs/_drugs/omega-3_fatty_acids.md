# Omega-3 Fatty Acids: From Hypertriglyceridaemia to Tendinitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Omega-3 fatty acids were originally used to treat hypertriglyceridaemia (high blood triglycerides).
The TxGNN model predicts they may be effective for **tendinitis**, but there are currently **no registered clinical trials** and only **5 publications** (scoping reviews, a narrative review, and a Mendelian randomization study), none showing a confirmed treatment effect.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this report. Based on general knowledge, EPA and DHA are precursors of specialised pro-resolving mediators (resolvins and protectins) and reduce pro-inflammatory eicosanoids. This is a plausible route to modulating tendon inflammation and matrix remodelling.

The original indication (high triglycerides) is a metabolic condition, while tendinitis is a musculoskeletal one. The link is therefore not through the original indication but through the anti-inflammatory biology of omega-3 fatty acids. This is a hypothesis only: the available evidence is a scoping review, a narrative article on rotator cuff tendinopathy, and a 2026 Mendelian randomization study on Achilles tendinitis risk. No interventional data confirm a therapeutic effect.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug Interactions**: 51 interactions were identified, with the 20 listed all rated Moderate. They are mainly with antiplatelet, anticoagulant and thrombolytic agents, consistent with a mild additive bleeding risk:
- Antiplatelets: acetylsalicylic acid, clopidogrel, cilostazol, abciximab, cangrelor
- Anticoagulants: apixaban, betrixaban, dalteparin, danaparoid, argatroban, bivalirudin, desirudin, antithrombin III
- Thrombolytics: alteplase, anistreplase
- Others: caplacizumab, defibrotide, dextran products

For other warnings and contraindications, please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI). Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, but the evidence is indirect (reviews and a genetic association study), with no registered trials and no interventional data. The existing registration is for an infusion product, which is not an obvious fit for tendinitis.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Randomised controlled trials of omega-3 fatty acids in tendinitis or tendinopathy
- Mechanism of action data and the SAHPRA package insert warnings and contraindications, which are currently missing and block safety screening
- Assessment of a suitable route and formulation, since only an infusion is registered
- Bleeding-risk review for patients on antiplatelets or anticoagulants

### Other Predicted Indications (for Reference)

Among the other top-ranked predictions, **fibromyalgia** (score 98.74%, L4) has the most supporting literature, including a 2000 clinical study of omega-3 in fibromyalgia syndrome whose design and size could not be verified. The rest are weakly supported or biologically doubtful: Glanzmann thrombasthenia, primary release disorder of platelets and pseudo-von Willebrand disease are bleeding disorders where the mild antiplatelet effect could worsen bleeding. Amenorrhoea, idiopathic granulomatous myositis and myositis fibrosa rest on model scores alone. Brain small vessel disease 1 has only observational data on sporadic cerebrovascular disease, which does not address this genetic disorder.
