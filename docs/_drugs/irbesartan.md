## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Irbesartan is an ARB, and blocking the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) is biologically plausible for hypertensive kidney injury. The model's prediction is likely driven by the closeness of this disease to other blood-pressure and kidney conditions that ARBs are known to affect.

There are important caveats:

- No trial or publication specifically studying irbesartan in malignant hypertensive renal disease was supplied, so this remains a computational prediction.
- Malignant hypertension is a hypertensive emergency. Oral ARB monotherapy is not standard care in that setting, and any role for irbesartan would more likely be longer-term kidney protection after blood pressure is controlled by other means.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug Interactions** (from the interaction data compiled for this report, 100 interactions in total; the main ones are listed):

- **Major:** potassium citrate and potassium bicarbonate. Combining them with an ARB can raise serum potassium, so monitor potassium and renal function.
- **Moderate:** corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, triamcinolone), acetylsalicylic acid, SGLT2 inhibitors (canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin), insulins (aspart, degludec), bupropion, aprepitant, morphine, dronabinol, and bowel-preparation agents (picosulfuric acid, sodium sulfate, polyethylene glycol with electrolytes).

Label warnings and contraindications were not available. Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for full safety information. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, but no trial or publication addresses malignant hypertensive renal disease (evidence level L5). Malignant hypertension is an emergency setting where oral ARB monotherapy is not standard care.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A systematic search for clinical or observational data on ARBs in malignant hypertensive nephropathy
- Mechanism-of-action data and the SAHPRA PI safety information (warnings and contraindications)
- Clarification of the clinical role, such as long-term renal protection after blood pressure control, as opposed to acute treatment
- Safety assessment for use in severe hypertension with impaired renal function, including potassium and renal monitoring
