# Leucovorin: From Folate Rescue Therapy to Primary Hyperoxaluria

## One-Sentence Summary

Leucovorin (folinic acid) is a reduced folate that bypasses the dihydrofolate reductase step. It is commonly used as a rescue agent alongside folate-antagonist chemotherapy.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Primary Hyperoxaluria**, but there are currently **0 clinical trials** and **0 publications** supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, leucovorin is a reduced folate that bypasses dihydrofolate reductase. Its established role is folate rescue, not treatment of an oxalate disorder.

Primary hyperoxaluria is a metabolic disease of glyoxylate and oxalate handling. The supplied data show no direct link between folate metabolism and this pathway. The high TxGNN score (rank 3230 among all predictions) most likely reflects proximity in the knowledge graph, not a demonstrated pharmacological rationale. The mechanistic case for this indication is therefore weak and unverified.
