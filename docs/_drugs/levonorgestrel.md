# Levonorgestrel: From Hormonal Contraception to Acne

## One-Sentence Summary

Levonorgestrel is a progestin (a synthetic female sex hormone) that is marketed in South Africa as oral tablets. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **acne**, but this comes from a knowledge-graph prediction. Only **1 probable randomised trial**, of a combination pill with ethinylestradiol, supports the direction, and none of the **5 registered clinical trials** tests levonorgestrel for acne.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, levonorgestrel is a 19-nortestosterone-derived progestin used in hormonal contraceptives, either alone or combined with ethinylestradiol. Its mechanistic fit for acne is indirect and its direction of effect is uncertain.

Acne is driven partly by androgens acting on the pilosebaceous unit. Combined oral contraceptives can improve acne by raising sex hormone binding globulin (SHBG) and lowering free testosterone. This benefit is usually attributed to the ethinylestradiol component. Levonorgestrel is relatively androgenic, so on its own it may worsen acne. Any acne benefit therefore comes from the combination product, not from the drug alone.

The very high TxGNN score reflects graph-based association only and does not establish clinical benefit.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The acne prediction rests on a graph score plus one probable RCT of a combination pill. The benefit there is usually attributed to ethinylestradiol, and levonorgestrel itself is androgenic. No trial tests levonorgestrel alone for acne. The other five predictions (Worth syndrome, pregnancy-associated osteoporosis, autosomal dominant neovascular inflammatory vitreoretinopathy, and two benign breast adenoses) have no trials or literature and no plausible mechanistic link, so they are also on Hold.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SAHPRA Professional Information warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data
- Confirmation of the design and full text of the 2002 EE/LNG acne trial, and whether it isolates a levonorgestrel effect
- Manual verification of NCT05570786
- A comparison against existing acne treatments in the South African setting
