# Levonorgestrel: From Postmenopausal Osteoporosis to Acne

## One-Sentence Summary

Levonorgestrel is a progestin that, according to the TxGNN knowledge graph, is linked to postmenopausal osteoporosis and osteoporosis. In South Africa it is marketed mainly as an oral contraceptive. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **acne**, but **no clinical trials** are registered and the **20 publications** identified are mostly reviews, with only one randomised trial and that trial tested a combination pill.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Levonorgestrel is a progestin, a synthetic hormone that acts on progesterone receptors. It is derived from the 19-nortestosterone family and retains some androgenic activity (PMID 7825629).

Acne is driven partly by androgens acting on the sebaceous glands. Combined oral contraceptives can improve acne by raising sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG) and lowering free testosterone. This benefit is mainly attributed to the estrogen component and to anti-androgenic progestins such as chlormadinone acetate (PMID 21895044), not to levonorgestrel itself.

One randomised, placebo-controlled trial (PMID 12196750) tested ethinyl estradiol 20 µg plus levonorgestrel 100 µg in moderate acne. That trial evaluated the combination, so it does not show an effect of levonorgestrel alone. Progestin-only products (intrauterine systems and implants) are associated with acne as a side effect rather than a treatment. The high TxGNN score is therefore plausible only for combination products and is not supported for levonorgestrel as a single agent.

The link to the original osteoporosis indication is a knowledge-graph association. It does not provide a mechanistic bridge to acne.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindication-related conditions (from the knowledge graph, not label text):**
Conditions linked to levonorgestrel as contraindications include:
- Hypertension
- Epilepsy
- Porphyria
- Kidney disease
- Liver disease
- Gallbladder disease
- Diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis and hyperglycaemia
- Blood coagulation disease
- Hypertriglyceridaemia
- Hypothyroidism
- Systemic lupus erythematosus
- Asthma
- Hypercalcaemia and related calcium or phosphorus disorders
- Anxiety, neurotic and dysthymic disorders

**Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were found, and the 20 listed in the compiled data are all moderate. They include antidiabetic agents (acarbose, canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, ertugliflozin, exenatide, linagliptin, liraglutide, nateglinide and several insulins). Others are aprepitant, clotrimazole, glycerol phenylbutyrate and linaclotide.

**Other points from the literature:**
- Combined oral contraceptives are associated with venous thromboembolism (PMID 10652979).
- Hormonal contraceptives carry a small breast cancer risk signal.
- Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for official warnings. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only controlled evidence for acne involves a combination pill whose benefit is mainly attributed to the estrogen component and to other progestins. Levonorgestrel is androgenic and progestin-only formats are associated with acne as a side effect. The prediction is not supported for levonorgestrel as a single agent, and the evidence level is L4.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SAHPRA Professional Information (PI) warnings and contraindications, which are currently missing and block safety screening
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Comparative trials of levonorgestrel alone versus placebo or an anti-androgenic progestin combination in acne
- A clear rationale for how an osteoporosis-linked drug relates to acne, since the knowledge-graph link to osteoporosis is not clinically established
