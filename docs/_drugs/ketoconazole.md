## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, ketoconazole is an azole antifungal, and its antifungal activity is well established. Mechanistically it may be applicable to acne, but this link is not confirmed by the supplied data.

Three routes are plausible from general pharmacology:

1. **Yeast suppression.** *Malassezia* (*Pityrosporum*) yeasts are implicated in acne-like folliculitis and neonatal pustulosis. Ketoconazole could help where yeast is part of the picture.
2. **Bacterial and lipase effects.** Laboratory studies report that ketoconazole inhibits the growth of *Cutibacterium (Propionibacterium) acnes*, the bacterium associated with acne vulgaris, and inhibits its lipase activity. This may matter given rising antibiotic resistance.
3. **Androgen lowering.** Ketoconazole inhibits steroid synthesis, which could matter in hyperandrogenic acne such as that seen in PCOS. This route relates to systemic use.

The high TxGNN score is a computational prediction, not clinical evidence. Pityrosporum folliculitis is often misdiagnosed as acne vulgaris, so any benefit seen in "acne" may partly reflect misdiagnosed yeast folliculitis.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence is at model-prediction and laboratory level (L4): one small, unreported trial of topical ketoconazole for acne and no randomised results. The safety review is also blocked because package insert information is missing. Systemic ketoconazole carries known liver and endocrine safety concerns, so any repurposing question should focus on topical use. A topical cream is already registered in South Africa, which makes the topical route feasible.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results of NCT07237763 (topical ketoconazole vs adapalene), and confirmation of the exact condition studied
- Larger randomised trials in well-defined acne vulgaris, ideally excluding Pityrosporum folliculitis
- Download and review of the SAHPRA package inserts for both registered products (blocking data gap)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Confirmation of the approved indications and EML status of the registered products
