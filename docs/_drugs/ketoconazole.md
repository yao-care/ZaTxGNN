## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data were not available in the data compiled for this report. The reasoning below rests on the general pharmacology of ketoconazole.

- **Antifungal action:** Ketoconazole inhibits Malassezia (Pityrosporum) yeast. These yeasts are linked to folliculitis and acne-like eruptions, including neonatal Malassezia pustulosis.
- **Antibacterial action:** Laboratory studies show that ketoconazole inhibits the growth of *Propionibacterium acnes* and its lipase activity. This enzyme drives sebum breakdown and skin inflammation in acne vulgaris.
- **Androgen effect:** At higher systemic doses, ketoconazole inhibits steroid-synthesis enzymes such as CYP17A1 and lowers androgen production. This could matter in hyperandrogenic acne, for example in polycystic ovary syndrome.
- **Anti-inflammatory activity:** Ketoconazole has some anti-inflammatory activity.

Systemic use for acne is limited by serious liver toxicity, adrenal suppression, QT prolongation and many drug interactions. A topical formulation is therefore the more plausible route. The high TxGNN score is a computational prediction and is not clinical evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions are recorded. The main major-level ones are **loperamide**, **triamcinolone** and **budesonide**. Moderate-level interactions include famotidine, ranitidine, omeprazole, rabeprazole, hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, pioglitazone, aprepitant, alosetron and several anticholinergics (atropine, hyoscyamine, methscopolamine).
- **Systemic exposure:** Systemic ketoconazole is known for liver toxicity, adrenal suppression and QT prolongation. These concerns apply mainly to oral use and are relevant if any systemic route is considered for acne.

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The only direct support for acne is laboratory work on *P. acnes* and one small registered topical trial without results. This corresponds to evidence level L4.
- The very high TxGNN score is not clinical evidence, and systemic use carries notable safety risks.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results of NCT07237763 (topical ketoconazole 2% versus adapalene 2%), including efficacy and tolerability.
- Additional randomised trials of topical ketoconazole in acne, ideally including South African patients.
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data and the SAHPRA Professional Information warnings, which were not available.
- Confirmation of the approved indication wording for Ketazol cream, to judge whether this would be an off-label use.
