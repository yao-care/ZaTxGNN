# Tetracycline: From Bacterial Infections to Punctate Epithelial Keratoconjunctivitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Tetracycline is a broad-spectrum antibiotic, originally used for infections such as urinary tract infection, bronchitis, acne, gonorrhoea, pneumonia and conjunctivitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis**, but there are currently **0 registered clinical trials** and only **1 publication** (a case report) for this indication, so the prediction rests mainly on the model alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, tetracycline is a tetracycline-class antibiotic, its efficacy in bacterial infections including conjunctivitis is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis.

Tetracycline is an established treatment for chlamydial conjunctivitis (e.g. trachoma and inclusion conjunctivitis). Punctate superficial keratitis can follow chlamydial infection, so the prediction is largely an extension of the existing antibacterial indication. Tetracyclines are also reported to have anti-inflammatory and matrix metalloproteinase (MMP)-inhibitory effects on the ocular surface, which could be relevant.

The single publication retrieved describes post-infectious keratitis. It is not a treatment study, so it does not show that tetracycline improves outcomes.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindication-related conditions (knowledge-graph signals, not label text):** The knowledge graph links tetracycline to caution or contraindication in conditions including liver failure and liver disease, kidney disease and kidney failure, uraemia, G6PD deficiency, neutropenia and agranulocytosis, epilepsy, diabetes and diabetic ketoacidosis, blood coagulation disorders, *Clostridium difficile* colitis, peripheral neuropathy, atrioventricular block and atrial fibrillation. Because these come from a knowledge graph and not from the approved label, they must be checked against the SAHPRA Professional Information (PI).

**Drug Interactions:** The interaction query returned 100 interactions; the first 20 are summarised here.
- **Major:** isotretinoin, acitretin and aminolevulinic acid (oral). Retinoids carry a recognised risk of raised intracranial pressure when combined with tetracyclines.
- **Moderate:** ethinylestradiol, amoxicillin, ampicillin, aluminium hydroxide, bismuth subcitrate potassium, sodium citrate, aminophylline, atracurium, atovaquone, balsalazide, attapulgite, asparaginase (*E. coli* and *Erwinia*) and topical aminolevulinic acid.
- **Minor:** acetazolamide, hydrochlorothiazide and amiloride.

**Topical ocular and otic use:** An in vitro study (PMID 37026784) reports tetracycline cytotoxicity in human tympanic membrane fibroblasts. Tetracyclines were less cytotoxic than quinolones in that study.

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for the full warnings and contraindications. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, but the only supporting publication is a descriptive 1992 case report, with no registered trials and no direct treatment evidence. The link is plausible mainly because tetracycline already treats chlamydial conjunctivitis. Product-level safety information for the SAHPRA-registered tablet is also missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The SAHPRA package insert (warnings, contraindications and approved indications) for registration L/20.1.1/0384
- Mechanism of action data
- Controlled clinical evidence in punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis, or at least in post-chlamydial keratoconjunctivitis
- Confirmation of an ocular route and formulation, since the registered product is an oral tablet only
- Review of the other predicted indications. Chronic rhinosinusitis has the largest body of literature, but it concerns doxycycline and other class members rather than tetracycline itself. The Phase 2 pilot NCT05219929 in post-treatment Lyme disease (tolerability and feasibility, n=60) is the only completed tetracycline-specific trial in the data compiled for this report.
