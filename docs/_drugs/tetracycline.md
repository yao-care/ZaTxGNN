## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in this Evidence Pack. Tetracycline is an antibacterial that inhibits the bacterial 30S ribosomal subunit. It is also reported to have anti-inflammatory and matrix metalloproteinase (MMP)-inhibiting effects on the ocular surface, which may be relevant to corneal epithelial disease.

Punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis is often linked to infection, especially chlamydial conjunctivitis, and tetracyclines are antichlamydial. The only supporting paper describes two patients who developed persistent punctate keratitis *after* oral tetracycline or doxycycline had cleared chlamydial follicular conjunctivitis. This is indirect. It shows the drug is used in the same clinical setting, not that it treats the keratitis.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The high model score is not backed by direct evidence. The only publication is a case report in which keratitis persisted after tetracycline treatment, and there are no trials. The only registered product is an oral tablet, and no ophthalmic route has been assessed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The SAHPRA Professional Information (PI) for warnings and contraindications, which blocks the safety screen
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Route compatibility assessment, since only an oral tablet is registered and ophthalmic use is unaddressed
- Any controlled clinical data on tetracycline in this condition

Among the other predicted indications in this pack, **chronic rhinosinusitis** (rank 3) has the strongest support. It is rated L2 and has one completed Phase 3 trial, but all trials tested doxycycline, not tetracycline. It may merit separate evaluation.
