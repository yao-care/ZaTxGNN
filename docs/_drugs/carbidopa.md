## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Carbidopa is a peripheral L-aromatic amino-acid decarboxylase (DDC) inhibitor. It has no antiparkinson action on its own and is given with levodopa. By blocking the conversion of levodopa to dopamine outside the brain, it increases the amount of levodopa that reaches the brain.

For the top-ranked prediction, Rasmussen encephalitis, a plausible link to carbidopa is not apparent. It is an immune-mediated chronic focal encephalitis with seizures, not primarily a dopamine-deficiency disorder. The score should be treated as a computational signal only.

Other predictions are mechanistically more plausible because they involve dopaminergic deficit or parkinsonism:
- **PLA2G6-associated neurodegeneration** (rank 2): adult-onset forms can present with levodopa-responsive parkinsonism. The evidence is limited to case reports.
- **Lewy body dementia** (rank 6): nigrostriatal dopamine loss produces parkinsonian motor features. Carbidopa/levodopa may help motor symptoms, but dopaminergic therapy can worsen hallucinations and psychosis.
- **MCT8 deficiency (Allan-Herndon-Dudley syndrome)–related X-linked syndromes** (ranks 9 and 10): altered dopamine metabolism and childhood parkinsonism have been described, with reported responses to levodopa/carbidopa. Whether these papers match the specific disease labels in the prediction is uncertain.
- **Paralysis agitans, juvenile, of Hunt** (rank 7): this is a historical term for juvenile parkinsonism. It largely duplicates the existing indications and is not a true repurposing candidate.

## Safety Considerations

SAHPRA package insert warnings and contraindications were not available. Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for safety information. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

The knowledge-graph drug-disease contraindication links (not label text) flag the following conditions, which should be verified against the PI:
- **Neurological and psychiatric:** epilepsy, drug-induced dyskinesia, psychotic and schizoaffective disorders, schizophreniform disorder, anxiety disorder, neurotic disorder, dysthymic disorder, mental disorder
- **Cardiovascular:** orthostatic hypotension, postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome, multiple system atrophy, myocardial infarction, coronary thrombosis, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease
- **Hepatic, renal and gastrointestinal:** liver failure, liver disease, kidney disease, gallbladder disease, gastric ulcer, peptic ulcer disease, diarrheal disease, pyoureter
- **Metabolic, ocular and other:** diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, open-angle glaucoma, melanoma, hemoglobinopathy

**Drug interactions:** The interaction query returned one pharmacological entry. Carbidopa inhibits its target, L-aromatic amino-acid decarboxylase (DDC), which is its intended mechanism. No drug-drug interactions with severity ratings were reported.

Orthostatic hypotension is of particular concern for Lewy body dementia and related autonomic disorders, given the blood pressure lowering effect of levodopa shown in the pharmacodynamic study above.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The top-ranked prediction, Rasmussen subacute encephalitis, has a high model score (98.43%) but no trials or literature, and no apparent mechanistic link to carbidopa. The evidence level is L5.
- Lewy body dementia, PLA2G6-associated neurodegeneration and MCT8 deficiency–related syndromes are more plausible but remain research questions (L4), supported only by indirect studies, case reports and small observational reports.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (warnings, contraindications and approved indications), as the missing safety data currently blocks safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- For Rasmussen encephalitis: a rationale from immunology and epilepsy experts on whether any link to carbidopa exists, followed by a targeted literature search
- For the more plausible dopaminergic candidates: controlled or well-designed observational data on carbidopa/levodopa efficacy and on hallucination and psychosis risk (Lewy body dementia)
- Clarification of whether the MCT8 deficiency papers apply to the X-linked intellectual disability disease labels predicted by the model
