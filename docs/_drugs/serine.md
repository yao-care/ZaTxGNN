## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Serine is a proteinogenic amino acid and is present in the SAHPRA-registered products listed below, which are parenteral nutrition and dialysis solutions. Its original indications come from knowledge graph links rather than from product labelling.

The data compiled for this report do not support a mechanistic link between serine and familial visceral myopathy. This is a rare genetic smooth-muscle disorder, and serine has no known disease-modifying role in it. The very high score is a graph-based prediction only. No trials or publications test serine for this condition.

Other top-ranked predictions are also supported only by the model. They include intestinal obstruction, several forms of intestinal pseudo-obstruction, neuronal intestinal dysplasia type B, angle-closure glaucoma, aqueous misdirection and traumatic glaucoma. Identical scores for some of them suggest a shared graph neighbourhood rather than independent evidence.

For the second-ranked prediction, intestinal obstruction, 17 publications were retrieved. They are keyword matches, not evidence for serine therapy. They cover Peutz-Jeghers syndrome (caused by variants in the serine/threonine kinase gene STK11), cohesinopathies, peritoneal mesothelioma and a parasite serine proteinase. For angle-closure glaucoma, the 5 publications concern the serine protease gene PRSS56 and the genetic causes of nanophthalmos. These papers refer to "serine" as a protein term, not to L-serine as a drug.
