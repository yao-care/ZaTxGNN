## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacology, MPA is a progestin, and fibrocystic breast changes are considered hormone-responsive. One review in the literature describes the condition as driven by estrogen predominance and relative progesterone deficiency. Progestins could therefore, in principle, oppose estrogen-driven epithelial proliferation.

The direction of effect is uncertain. Several retrieved studies concern hormone replacement therapy (HRT) and breast changes such as mammographic density and epithelial proliferation, where progestin-containing regimens may worsen rather than improve breast findings. A review also notes that MPA can cause severe myoepithelial hyperplasia in beagle dogs. The mechanistic rationale is therefore plausible but unproven, and it carries a safety and directionality concern.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
No clinical trials exist and no cited study provides robust evidence in this disease, so the evidence level is L5 despite the high model score. The available human reports are small and dated, and the HRT literature raises the possibility that progestins may worsen breast findings.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Full-text review of the two older MPA studies in fibrocystic breast disease to confirm design, outcomes and safety
- Mechanism of action data for MPA
- SAHPRA Professional Information warnings and contraindications, particularly regarding breast cancer risk
- A well-designed controlled study if clinical interest continues
