## Medroxyprogesterone Acetate: Breast fibrocystic disease
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Medroxyprogesterone acetate (MPA) is a synthetic progestin marketed in South Africa as injectable and oral products. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **breast fibrocystic disease**, with **no registered clinical trials** and **9 publications**, of which two small older clinical reports of MPA in fibrocystic breast disease are the most direct. The evidence level is **L3**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. MPA is a progestin, and fibrocystic breast changes are considered hormone-responsive. One review in the literature describes the condition as driven by estrogen predominance and progesterone deficiency, so a progestin could in theory oppose estrogen-driven epithelial proliferation.

Two older clinical reports describe MPA used directly in fibrocystic breast disease: a 1983 therapeutic trial in severe disease and a 1988 study in adolescents. Both are small and of unclear design, and no randomized trial is available. Other publications concern breast changes during hormone replacement therapy (mammographic density, epithelial proliferation). In those settings progestins may worsen rather than improve breast findings, so the direction of effect is uncertain.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were found. All 20 listed are of moderate severity, mainly with antidiabetic agents (e.g. metformin, acarbose, glimepiride, semaglutide, liraglutide, insulin products, canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin), as well as dexamethasone, aprepitant and clarithromycin.
- **Literature signals:** The HRT literature suggests progestins can have mixed or proliferative effects on breast epithelium, which needs careful assessment in any breast indication.

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for warnings and contraindications. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is high, and two small older clinical reports of MPA in fibrocystic breast disease support an L3 evidence level. However, there are no registered trials or randomized evidence, the study designs are unclear, and related HRT data raise doubts about the direction of effect on breast tissue.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Full-text review of the 1983 and 1988 clinical reports to judge design, outcomes and safety
- Mechanism of action data for MPA
- SAHPRA Professional Information warnings and contraindications
- A well-designed controlled study, with a breast safety assessment, before any clinical use
