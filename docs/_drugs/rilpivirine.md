# Rilpivirine: From HIV Infection to Feline Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Rilpivirine is a non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NNRTI) originally used to treat HIV infection and AIDS.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **feline acquired immunodeficiency syndrome**, but this is supported by only **1 in vitro biochemical/structural publication** and **no registered clinical trials**. This is a veterinary indication with limited relevance to human drug repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, rilpivirine is an NNRTI that inhibits HIV-1 reverse transcriptase, and its efficacy in HIV has been established.

Feline immunodeficiency virus (FIV) is a lentivirus with a related reverse transcriptase, so there is a plausible mechanistic basis for the prediction. However, the only available study (2023) compared NNRTIs against FIV and HIV reverse transcriptase in biochemical and structural experiments. This is indirect, in vitro evidence, and NNRTIs are generally only weakly active against FIV. There is no in vivo or clinical evidence in cats.

Other predictions with a high score are worth noting for context:
- **Simian immunodeficiency virus infection** (score 99.97%): several macaque studies of long-acting rilpivirine, alone or with cabotegravir, cover treatment, post-exposure prophylaxis and remission. This is preclinical support for the existing HIV indication rather than a new indication.
- **AIDS related complex** (99.56%): a historical term for symptomatic HIV disease, already within the approved indication.
- **Congenital HIV** (99.56%): the literature is mainly about long-acting cabotegravir/rilpivirine in pregnancy. It addresses pregnancy safety and pharmacokinetics, not treatment of infants.
- **A rare neurodevelopmental disorder with ataxic gait, absent speech and decreased cortical white matter** (99.97%): no trials, no literature and no plausible mechanism. This is likely a network artefact of the model.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only evidence for feline AIDS is a single in vitro study, with no animal or clinical data, and NNRTIs are generally weakly active against FIV. The indication is veterinary, so it does not translate into a human repurposing opportunity in South Africa. The other high-scoring predictions are either already covered by the approved HIV use or have no supporting evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SAHPRA Professional Information (warnings, contraindications and approved indications), which is currently missing and blocks safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank)
- For any veterinary pursuit: in vivo efficacy and safety data in cats and a veterinary regulatory pathway
- For human relevance: focus on the existing HIV indication, such as long-acting cabotegravir/rilpivirine, pregnancy and paediatric data, rather than on this prediction
