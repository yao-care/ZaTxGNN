# Magnesium Trisilicate: From Peptic Ulcer Disease to Active Peptic Ulcer Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Magnesium trisilicate is an antacid originally used for peptic ulcer disease, gastroesophageal reflux disease, oesophagitis and dyspepsia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **active peptic ulcer disease**, but this is essentially the same as its existing indication rather than true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, magnesium trisilicate is an antacid that neutralises gastric acid. Its use in acid-related upper gastrointestinal disease is long established, and mechanistically it is applicable to active peptic ulcer disease.

The predicted indication is essentially the same as the drug's existing indication. The very high prediction score most likely reflects this close overlap in the knowledge graph, so the prediction should not be read as a new therapeutic discovery.

Other predicted conditions follow the same pattern. Gastric ulcer falls within the peptic ulcer class. Gastroduodenitis is similar to the existing dyspepsia and oesophagitis indications, although no studies were retrieved for it. Gastrojejunal ulcer (an anastomotic ulcer) shares acid-peptic pathogenesis, but the evidence is indirect. Peptic ulcer perforation is a surgical emergency that antacids do not treat. At most, antacids could support ulcer management after surgery.

## Safety Considerations

The data compiled for this report contain no label-based warnings, contraindications or drug interaction records. Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for safety information. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

The knowledge graph lists the following conditions as contraindications (these are graph associations, not label text, and should be verified against the PI):
- Kidney disease
- Congestive heart failure
- Hypophosphataemia
- Diarrhoeal disease (acute and chronic)
- Constipation disorder and intestinal impaction
- Haemorrhoids
- Pyoureter

Signals from the retrieved literature:
- Antacids containing aluminium and magnesium can reduce the absorption of co-administered drugs such as phenytoin and norethisterone. Dosing should be separated from other oral medicines.
- Silica-containing urinary stones have been reported as a rare clinical issue, so prolonged high-dose use warrants caution.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top predictions largely restate the drug's existing antacid indications rather than a new use. There are no registered trials, and the literature is historical, mostly combination-based and indirect. The registered South African product has no indication text or safety data available, so the evidence does not support a repurposing initiative.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The SAHPRA package insert (indications, warnings, contraindications), which is a blocking gap for safety screening
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A clear determination of whether any prediction is genuinely new, or only reflects the existing labelled indications
- Modern, direct evidence for magnesium trisilicate as a single agent, if a new indication is pursued
- Confirmation of EML status and of the registered product's approved indication
