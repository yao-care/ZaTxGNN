# Simvastatin: From Hypertriglyceridaemia and Atherosclerotic Disease to Familial Hypercholesterolaemia

## One-Sentence Summary

Simvastatin is a statin (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor) originally used for lipid disorders and atherosclerotic or cerebrovascular disease, including homozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **familial hypercholesterolaemia**, with **8 clinical trials** and **20 publications** compiled for this report.
Because homozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia is already among its original indications, this is an on-label or near-label use rather than true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Simvastatin inhibits HMG-CoA reductase, which reduces cholesterol synthesis in the liver and upregulates LDL receptors. This lowers LDL cholesterol (LDL-C) and is the core mechanism for treating familial hypercholesterolaemia. Detailed mechanism of action data was not available in the data compiled for this report, so this explanation draws on established pharmacology.

The original indications already include homozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia, hyperlipoproteinaemia and familial combined hyperlipidaemia. The predicted indication is therefore closely related to existing uses. Efficacy may be reduced in patients with severe LDL receptor defects, which is why combination therapy (for example with ezetimibe) is often needed.

## Safety Considerations

Label warnings and contraindications from the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please consult the PI before use.

**Contraindications and cautions from the knowledge graph (not label text):** The knowledge graph links simvastatin to the following conditions, which should be verified against the PI:
- Liver disease, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease
- Myopathy, muscle disease, rhabdomyolysis
- Diabetes mellitus, hyperglycaemia, diabetic ketoacidosis
- Thrombocytopenia, blood coagulation disease
- Acute coronary syndrome, myocardial infarction, coronary artery disease
- Gout, peptic ulcer disease, hypotensive disorder, substance abuse/dependence, hypophosphataemia, and others

**Drug interactions:** The interaction screen returned 100 interacting drugs. The major-level interactions shown include:
- **Amiodarone** (Major)
- **Amlodipine** (Major)
- **Fenofibrate** (Major)

Moderate interactions include nifedipine, omeprazole, paclitaxel, ethanol, zidovudine, aprepitant and apalutamide.

**HIV context:** Protease inhibitors and other CYP3A4 inhibitors raise simvastatin exposure and myopathy risk. Case reports describe rhabdomyolysis in people on antiretroviral therapy. This is especially relevant in South Africa given the HIV burden.

Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple completed Phase 3 trials (including ENHANCE, with 720 participants) and Cochrane reviews support simvastatin-based therapy in familial hypercholesterolaemia, and the drug is already marketed in South Africa. Because this is essentially an existing indication, the main concerns are safety verification and the missing local label data.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SAHPRA package insert (PI) warnings, contraindications and approved indications, which are currently missing
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- A review of the ENHANCE trial result, which did not show an added carotid benefit from ezetimibe despite greater LDL-C lowering
- Dose limits and monitoring for muscle toxicity and liver enzymes, especially with CYP3A4-inhibiting interacting drugs
- Specific guidance for patients on antiretroviral therapy
- Paediatric dosing guidance, if use in children and adolescents is planned
- Confirmation of EML status and local clinical guideline alignment
