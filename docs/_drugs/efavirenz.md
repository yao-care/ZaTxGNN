# Efavirenz: From HIV Infection to Simian Immunodeficiency Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Efavirenz was originally used to treat HIV infection and AIDS. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **simian immunodeficiency virus infection**, but this prediction is supported only by **0 clinical trials** and **14 publications**, mostly animal (macaque) and laboratory studies. It is therefore an extension of the existing HIV indication, not a new human use.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Efavirenz is known to be a non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NNRTI) of HIV-1, and its efficacy in HIV infection is established.

Wild-type SIV is intrinsically resistant to NNRTIs. However, the RT-SHIV chimera (SIV carrying the HIV-1 reverse transcriptase gene) is susceptible to efavirenz. The supporting studies use this chimera in macaques as a preclinical model of HIV-1 therapy.

The high TxGNN score most likely reflects how closely SIV infection resembles HIV infection. It does not point to a new therapeutic use in people, so this prediction has no human clinical repurposing value.

Two other predictions were also reviewed:
- **Feline acquired immunodeficiency syndrome:** one in vitro and structural study compared NNRTIs against feline immunodeficiency virus (FIV) reverse transcriptase (PMID [38031646](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38031646/)). It has no efficacy data in cats and is of limited veterinary relevance.
- **Neurodevelopmental disorder with ataxic gait, absent speech, and decreased cortical white matter (score 99.77%):** no trials or literature were found, and no plausible mechanism is supported. Efavirenz's known CNS adverse effects argue for caution in this population. This is a hypothesis-generating signal only.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication signals from the knowledge graph (not label text):** liver failure, liver disease, fatty liver disease, non-alcoholic steatohepatitis, kidney disease, Fanconi renotubular syndrome, epilepsy, anxiety disorder, neurotic disorder, dysthymic disorder, delusional disorder, paranoid schizophrenia, chronic hepatitis B and C virus infection, hypertriglyceridemia, obesity, lactic acidosis, hypophosphatemia, osteomalacia, rickets, and gallbladder disease. These should be checked against the approved label.
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were identified. One is Major: **dolasetron**. Many are Moderate, including acarbose, famotidine, rabeprazole, bupropion, aprepitant, clarithromycin, saxagliptin, pioglitazone, loperamide, minocycline, dronabinol, and the corticosteroids hydrocortisone, triamcinolone, dexamethasone and betamethasone.
- **CNS effects:** efavirenz has known neuropsychiatric adverse effects, which matters for any neurological or neurodevelopmental use.

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications, and report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The evidence is preclinical (L4): macaque and laboratory studies using an engineered SIV chimera, with no human trials. The prediction extends the existing HIV indication and offers no new human therapeutic use. Efavirenz is already widely marketed in South Africa for HIV.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Any rationale for a human or veterinary use distinct from HIV-1 treatment
- Mechanism of action data
- SAHPRA package insert warnings and contraindications, which have not been obtained and block safety screening
- For the neurodevelopmental disorder prediction, a plausible mechanism and a CNS safety assessment before any further consideration
