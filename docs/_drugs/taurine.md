# Taurine: From Parenteral Nutrition Component to Alcohol Withdrawal Delirium

## One-Sentence Summary

Taurine is registered in South Africa only as an ingredient of parenteral nutrition infusions (Numeta and SmofKabiven products), and the data compiled for this report do not state a separate approved indication for it.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **alcohol withdrawal delirium**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** support this specific prediction.
The best-supported alternative among the model's top predictions is **migraine disorder**, with observational literature only and no trials.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not currently available for taurine. Taurine is an inhibitory neuromodulator, and it is thought to act at GABA-A and glycine receptors. It may also dampen glutamate-driven excitotoxicity.

Alcohol withdrawal delirium is a state of marked neuronal hyperexcitability, caused by reduced GABA tone and excess glutamate activity after alcohol is stopped. This is the conceptual link behind the prediction. However, taurine's registered use is nutritional, and no trials or publications were found that test this link. **The mechanistic rationale is therefore hypothetical.**

**Note on other predicted indications:** Migraine disorder (TxGNN score 89.2%) has the most developed literature among the model's top 10 predictions. It is still observational and biomarker-based, with no registered trials and no RCT of taurine alone.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction for alcohol withdrawal delirium rests on the model score alone (L5). There are no trials or publications, and the mechanistic link is hypothetical. Local registrations are for intravenous nutrition products, so there is also no confirmed oral or other route suited to this use.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The SAHPRA Professional Information (PI) for the four registered products, to obtain warnings, contraindications and approved indications. This is a blocking gap for any safety screening.
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data, for example from DrugBank.
- A targeted literature search for taurine in alcohol withdrawal and delirium, as well as a trial registry search.
- An assessment of route compatibility, since only infusion products are registered locally.
- Consideration of migraine as a better-supported research question. This would need a systematic review and, ideally, a well-designed trial of taurine alone.
