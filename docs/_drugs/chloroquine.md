## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Chloroquine is generally described as an immunomodulator that acts through lysosomal and autophagy inhibition, reduced antigen presentation and inhibition of Toll-like receptor signalling. These are mechanisms shared with the antimalarial hydroxychloroquine, and they are relevant to autoimmune joint disease.

Rheumatoid factor-positive polyarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis closely resembles adult rheumatoid arthritis, a disease in which chloroquine has long been used. The high TxGNN score likely reflects this disease similarity. This is a mechanistic and network-based argument only. No study in the data directly tests chloroquine in this condition.

## Safety Considerations

- **Conditions flagged as contraindications or cautions in drug-disease relations:** epilepsy, porphyria (including inherited porphyria), liver disease, gallbladder disease, psoriasis, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, leukopenia, anaemia, G6PD deficiency, substance abuse/dependence, and eye, retinal, vision, hearing and auditory disorders. These are not taken from the SAHPRA label text and must be checked against the Professional Information (PI).
- **Cardiac signal in the literature:** a case report (PMID 9523387) describes third-degree atrioventricular block in a patient with juvenile chronic arthritis on long-term chloroquine.
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were found. Major interactions include agalsidase beta, bupropion, cisapride, clarithromycin, dolasetron and granisetron. Moderate interactions include famotidine, cimetidine, loperamide, metronidazole, aprepitant, antacids (aluminium hydroxide, calcium carbonate), attapulgite, insulin and sulfonylureas (glimepiride, glipizide, glyburide, chlorpropamide, acetohexamide).

Warnings and contraindications from the SAHPRA-approved PI have not been retrieved. Please refer to the PI and report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.
