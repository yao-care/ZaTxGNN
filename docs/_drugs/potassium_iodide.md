## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, potassium iodide has long been used as an expectorant and as a treatment for certain fungal infections such as sporotrichosis. Its effect in infectious nasal disease is plausibly antimicrobial or immunomodulatory, but this is not established.

The available reports describe fungal or oomycete infections of the nose and face: rhinofacial pythiosis in sheep, mycotic rhinitis and a *Pseudallescheria boydii* infection in horses, and one human case of nasofacial zygomycosis. These are specific infectious diseases, not nasal cavity disease in general. The very high TxGNN score therefore overstates what the evidence supports. The link to the original indications (rhinitis, pharyngitis, common cold) is a network-based association and has not been shown clinically.

**Drug Interactions** (45 recorded; 20 retrieved are listed below by severity):
- **Major**: ACE inhibitors (benazepril, captopril, enalapril, lisinopril, moexipril, perindopril, quinapril), angiotensin receptor blockers (olmesartan, valsartan, azilsartan medoxomil, losartan, telmisartan) and trimethoprim. These are likely linked to hyperkalaemia risk.
- **Moderate**: NSAIDs (ketorolac, ibuprofen, diclofenac, oxaprozin, naproxen), aliskiren and tolvaptan.

**Other points:**
- Iodides can cause hypersensitivity reactions, including urticaria.
- Thyroid effects are a recognised concern, as shown by the iontophoresis study (PMID 34341406) that examined thyroid parameters.

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for full safety information. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high (0.9995), but the supporting evidence is limited to veterinary case reports and a single human case of fungal infection. There are no registered trials and no controlled human data. The locally registered products are infusion formulations, not suited to nasal use. Safety data from the product label are also missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clarify the target population: specific fungal infections of the nose and face (for example, zygomycosis or sporotrichosis) rather than "nasal cavity disease" in general
- Systematic review of human evidence for potassium iodide in fungal rhinosinusitis and rhinofacial mycoses
- Detailed mechanism of action data (MOA)
- SAHPRA package insert warnings and contraindications, which are currently a blocking data gap
- Assessment of a suitable oral or other formulation, since only infusion products are registered in South Africa
- Safety monitoring plan covering thyroid function, potassium levels and hypersensitivity
