## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

According to the Clinical Pharmacology section of the US FDA-approved prescribing information for acetic acid: "Acetic acid is antibacterial and antifungal; propylene glycol is hydrophilic and provides a low surface tension; benzethonium chloride is a surface active agent that promotes contact of the solution with tissues." ([DailyMed: acetic acid prescribing information, A-S Medication Solutions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=c1c28375-9b03-4590-9d39-ab28403da93d), label version 2026-03-18; accessed 2026-11-19.) Acetic acid is generally known as a topical acidifying and antibacterial agent, which acts by lowering local pH and disrupting microbial growth.

"Post-bacterial disorder" is a vague category. It is not clear which condition it covers, and no specific mechanistic link between acetic acid and sequelae that follow a bacterial infection can be supported from the data compiled for this report. A topical antibacterial effect would act on the bacteria during active infection, not on the later sequelae. The prediction should therefore be treated as a hypothesis generated by the model, not as an established rationale.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has a very high model score (0.9998) but no clinical trials, no publications and no plausible mechanistic link. The evidence level is L5, and the safety data from the package insert are missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A clear definition of what "post-bacterial disorder" covers clinically, and a testable mechanistic hypothesis
- Mechanism of action data for acetic acid
- SAHPRA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- A literature and trial search that targets the defined condition
- Confirmation that an appropriate route and formulation exist, since the registered products are dialysis-related solutions and infusions
