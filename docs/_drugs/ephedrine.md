# Ephedrine: From Cold, Flu and Cough Combination Products to Nasal Cavity Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Ephedrine is a sympathomimetic drug. In South Africa it is registered mainly in oral cold, flu and cough products, although the supplied record does not state an approved indication.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **nasal cavity disease**.
The evidence is thin: the trials listed are only loosely related, and none clearly tests ephedrine. **1 randomised study** compares a related decongestant (pseudoephedrine), and the remaining publications are animal models or older reports.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not in the supplied record, so this reasoning rests on general pharmacology. Ephedrine is a mixed-acting sympathomimetic. It releases norepinephrine and directly stimulates alpha-adrenergic receptors. This constricts the blood vessels of the nasal lining and reduces swelling and congestion.

This is consistent with the long-standing use of ephedrine and pseudoephedrine as nasal decongestants. Nasal congestion is central to many nasal cavity diseases, so the prediction is biologically plausible.

The high TxGNN score is a model output only. It is not clinical proof. Systemic effects (raised blood pressure, fast heart rate, CNS stimulation) would also need review for any new use.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for safety information. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

Ephedrine's sympathomimetic effects (hypertension, tachycardia, CNS stimulation) warrant a formal safety review before any new use. This is general pharmacology, not registration-specific data. No drug interaction records were found.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible, but no trial in the pack clearly tests ephedrine for nasal cavity disease. The strongest human evidence is one RCT of a related drug (pseudoephedrine). The SAHPRA safety data is missing, which blocks safety screening. This is currently a research question, not a candidate for action.

The two lower-ranked predictions (acute laryngopharyngitis, 99.90%; trigeminal autonomic cephalalgia, 99.34%) have no trials or literature. For trigeminal autonomic cephalalgia, ephedrine's stimulant effects could plausibly worsen headache, so it should not be pursued on current evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SAHPRA package insert warnings and contraindications (download and parse the PI PDF), which is the blocking gap
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Approved indication text for the 13 SAHPRA registrations
- Verification of whether ephedrine is an arm in NCT00562120 and NCT03620513
- Ephedrine-specific clinical evidence in nasal congestion or nasal cavity disease
- A cardiovascular and CNS safety review for the intended population
