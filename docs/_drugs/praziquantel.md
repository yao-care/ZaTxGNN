# Praziquantel: From Opisthorchiasis and Schistosomiasis to Plasmodium falciparum Malaria

## One-Sentence Summary

Praziquantel is an anthelmintic originally used to treat opisthorchiasis, schistosomiasis, enterobiasis and trichinosis.
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is uterine corpus epithelioid leiomyosarcoma, which has no supporting evidence. The best-supported candidate is **Plasmodium falciparum malaria**, with **2 clinical trials** (both indirect, co-infection studies) and **20 publications**, including one 2026 randomised trial of praziquantel against P. falciparum infection.
This report therefore focuses on malaria, the most evidence-rich prediction, and notes the other predictions briefly.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. From general pharmacology, praziquantel acts on schistosome calcium channels (TRPM_PZQ) and damages the parasite's outer surface (tegument). That makes it effective against flukes and tapeworms.

There is no established direct antimalarial mechanism. Praziquantel is not a known plasmodicidal agent. Schistosomiasis and malaria are often co-endemic in sub-Saharan Africa, so much of the literature is epidemiological or indirect. Treating helminths may alter malaria susceptibility or transmission.

Two items point toward a possible direct effect:
- A 2026 double-blind, placebo-controlled randomised trial (PMID 41159886) assessed praziquantel for P. falciparum infection in asymptomatic Gabonese adults. Its design and results need full-text verification.
- A small 1998 case series (PMID 10531774) reported cures in 8 of 10 patients. It is old, uncontrolled and weak evidence.

The high TxGNN score for malaria is therefore best treated as a hypothesis, not as evidence of efficacy.

**Other predictions:** The top-ranked predictions are mostly sarcomas (uterine corpus epithelioid and myxoid leiomyosarcoma, retroperitoneal sarcoma, anus leiomyosarcoma, small intestinal sarcoma, leiomyosarcoma, dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans). They have no mechanistic link and no clinical evidence, and are likely driven by graph similarity. All are Level L5 and recommended Hold.
- **Gnathostomiasis (L4):** The only drug-specific item is a case report of a worm emerging after praziquantel treatment, which does not show efficacy. Praziquantel is also not an established treatment.
- **Fascioliasis (L4):** Praziquantel is biologically plausible against this trematode, but clinical reports suggest poor efficacy. Triclabendazole is the standard of care.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (research question only)**

**Rationale:**
- The TxGNN scores are high, but there is no established antimalarial mechanism. Most malaria evidence is epidemiological or indirect. The highest-ranked prediction (leiomyosarcoma) and the other sarcoma predictions have no supporting evidence. Gnathostomiasis and fascioliasis have better established alternatives.
- Praziquantel is already marketed in South Africa, but any malaria use would be off-label and unsupported by current guidelines.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Full-text review of the 2026 Gabon randomised trial (PMID 41159886): design, parasitological endpoints, effect size and replication.
- Mechanistic studies of any direct antiplasmodial activity.
- SAHPRA Professional Information (indications, warnings, contraindications) for the two registered products, and EML status.
- Dose-finding and safety data for P. falciparum, and an assessment of interactions with antimalarials and anticonvulsants common in co-endemic settings.
