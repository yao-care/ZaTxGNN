## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Lamivudine is a nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NRTI) with activity against HIV-1 reverse transcriptase and HBV polymerase. Its efficacy in HIV and hepatitis B is established.

Feline immunodeficiency virus (FIV) is a lentivirus related to HIV, with a similar reverse transcriptase. Many HIV antiretrovirals also act against FIV, which is why cats are used as an animal model of HIV. The prediction is therefore mechanistically plausible.

However, the evidence is veterinary only. Human trials do not apply, and the human equivalent (HIV) is already an approved use. This is not a true repurposing opportunity for human medicine.

Other predictions in the list are weaker:
- **Simian immunodeficiency virus infection** (score 99.93%): macaque studies model HIV and do not represent a new human indication. Several retrieved papers are off-topic (HIV epidemiology in Ivory Coast, reviews of other antivirals).
- **Chronic hepatitis C virus infection** (score 99.11%): lamivudine has no meaningful activity against HCV, an RNA virus that uses a different polymerase. The literature is general viral hepatitis reviews in which lamivudine is discussed for HBV. Direct-acting antivirals already cure most patients.
- **A rare neurodevelopmental disorder** and **obsolete familial combined hyperlipidaemia**: no mechanistic link, no trials and no literature. These are likely knowledge-graph artefacts.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The top predictions restate an already approved HIV indication in animal models (cat and macaque), or have no supporting evidence. There are no clinical trials, and the evidence level is L4 at best (L5 for the rare neurodevelopmental and hyperlipidaemia predictions). None represents a genuine new human repurposing opportunity.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SAHPRA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for any safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data
- Approved indication text for SAHPRA registrations
- Confirmation of whether a veterinary indication is of interest; if so, evaluation falls under veterinary regulation rather than human drug repurposing
