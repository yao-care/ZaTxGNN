# Lamivudine: From Antiviral Therapy to Feline Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Lamivudine is a nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NRTI) antiviral that is already marketed in South Africa. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **feline acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (FIV infection in cats)**, which is a veterinary disease and not a human indication. Support comes from **5 listed clinical trials, none of which studied FIV** (all are human HIV-1 studies), and **5 publications** (in vitro, animal and cohort studies in cats).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Lamivudine belongs to the NRTI class, which blocks the reverse transcriptase enzyme that retroviruses use to copy their genetic material. FIV is a lentivirus, in the same family as HIV, and depends on the same enzyme. This makes activity against FIV biologically plausible.

The animal literature supports this link. Studies show that reverse transcriptase inhibitors active against HIV are also active against FIV. This is why the FIV-infected cat is used as an animal model of HIV. Lamivudine (3TC) has been tested against FIV mainly in combination with zidovudine (AZT), with or without abacavir.

This prediction is not a human repurposing candidate. FIV affects cats, and the retrieved human trials show only lamivudine's established antiretroviral activity, not efficacy in FIV. The Evidence Pack also lists no original indications for lamivudine, so the mapping to its original use is unverified.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The FIV prediction rests on a plausible mechanism and small animal and in vitro studies, and no trial tests lamivudine in FIV. FIV is a veterinary disease, so it is not a human repurposing indication. The lower-ranked predictions in the pack (SIV infection, a rare neurodevelopmental disorder, an obsolete hyperlipidaemia term) have no supporting evidence. The chronic hepatitis C prediction is also not supported: lamivudine has no established activity against HCV, and the retrieved evidence mostly concerns hepatitis B.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirmation of the intended use: veterinary FIV research, or a human indication after re-screening of the prediction list
- Lamivudine's original indications and mechanism of action, which are missing from the pack
- SAHPRA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Manual review of NCT06356740 (abacavir/lamivudine in systemic lupus erythematosus, Phase 2, not yet recruiting), the only trial that tests lamivudine directly. It was retrieved under the HCV prediction and is not linked to FIV.
