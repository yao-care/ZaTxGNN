## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Copper is an essential trace element and a cofactor for enzymes such as lysyl oxidase and ceruloplasmin, and copper status has been loosely linked to platelet function and haemostasis.

However, no direct, validated pathway connects copper supplementation to correcting a platelet release (secretion) defect, which is typically caused by granule or signalling abnormalities. The link is speculative and is not established by the data compiled for this report.

Two other model outputs (pseudo-von Willebrand disease and immune thrombocytopenia) are also weak on mechanism. Pseudo-von Willebrand disease is a receptor-level genetic defect (GP1BA gain-of-function) that copper would not be expected to correct. Immune platelet destruction is a different process from the cytopenias caused by copper deficiency.
