## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this drug. Copper is an essential micronutrient involved in enzyme function, antioxidant defence and connective-tissue biology, but the data compiled for this report do not describe a mechanism that links it to the predicted conditions.

**Esotropia (rank 1):** Esotropia is an eye-alignment (oculomotor/neuromuscular) disorder. No supporting data exist, and no plausible mechanistic link to copper is evident. The high score is a model output only.

**Dermatitis (rank 2):** There are some biologically plausible routes, but the direction of effect is unclear:
- Copper acts as a contact allergen (copper hypersensitivity reviews).
- Zinc and copper status is altered in atopic and seborrheic dermatitis.
- Preclinical work suggests cuproptosis-related pathways regulate chronic skin inflammation (PMID 41064128).
- A preclinical copper-based acidic hydrogel for atopic dermatitis has been developed (PMID 42565357).

In the current data, copper appears more often as an allergen or dysregulated biomarker than as a proven treatment. Any repurposing would need to show a therapeutic rather than harmful effect.

**Other candidates (ranks 3–10)** have little or no supporting evidence. Dry eye syndrome has only indirect preclinical links (antioxidant copper-selenide nanoparticles in a different formulation from the marketed products).

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for safety information. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

Relevant points from the literature: copper can cause contact sensitisation and hypersensitivity reactions, which is directly relevant to any dermatological use. Neonatal use (as proposed for some predictions) would require additional safety consideration.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top prediction (esotropia) is model output only, with no trials, literature or plausible mechanism. The dermatitis prediction has more literature, but it is mostly associative or preclinical, the two trials provide no efficacy data, and copper appears more often as an allergen than a treatment. This is best treated as a research question rather than a candidate for clinical action.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SAHPRA package insert warnings, contraindications and approved indications (currently missing; blocks safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data
- Clarification of the Spiriva Respimat registration listing
- For dermatitis: controlled human studies showing a therapeutic (not harmful) effect of copper, with a defined formulation and route (topical vs. systemic), plus assessment of sensitisation risk
- Route-compatibility assessment, since marketed forms are infusion, TPN and oral products rather than topical or ophthalmic
