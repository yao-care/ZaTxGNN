# Mycophenolate Mofetil: From Transplant Immunosuppression to HIV Infectious Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Mycophenolate mofetil (MMF) is an immunosuppressant that is generally used to prevent organ transplant rejection. The SAHPRA registration record supplied does not state its approved indication.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **HIV infectious disease**.
The search retrieved **10 clinical trials** and **20 publications**, but only a few are directly relevant, and there is no completed Phase 3 confirmation.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the source record. Based on the assessment in the evidence pack, the active metabolite, mycophenolic acid, inhibits the enzyme IMPDH. This depletes guanosine nucleotides and limits lymphocyte proliferation.

In HIV, chronic immune activation is linked to CD4 T-cell depletion and disease progression. Fewer activated CD4+ T cells could mean fewer cells to support viral replication. Depleting intracellular deoxyguanosine triphosphate (dGTP) may also boost the activity of guanosine-analogue antiretrovirals such as abacavir and didanosine. Laboratory and small clinical studies have explored this idea.

The mechanism is plausible but double-edged. Immunosuppression in a person with HIV carries a risk of opportunistic infection. The available Phase 4 trials, pilot studies and pharmacokinetic studies mainly provide safety and PK information. None shows an established virological or clinical benefit.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

Ten trials were retrieved. The table lists the five most relevant, plus three that give only indirect information. The other two, NCT06869265 (a haploidentical transplant conditioning study in acute myeloid leukaemia) and NCT01288131 (anti-EPO pure red cell aplasia), are off-target and omitted. Trials NCT02793544 and NCT01288131 and other transplant or conditioning studies are also off-target for HIV. No SANCTR or PACTR identifiers were found in the pack.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for safety information. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

The one safety point from the evidence assessment is that immunosuppression in people with HIV carries a risk of opportunistic infection.

---

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible, and small pilot and PK studies exist. However, there is no completed Phase 3 trial, no established virological or clinical benefit, and a real infection risk in an already immunocompromised population. The safety information from the SAHPRA package insert is also missing, so safety screening cannot proceed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The SAHPRA Professional Information (warnings, contraindications, interactions), to allow safety screening
- Confirmation of the approved indication for Mycocept (Reg. No. 41/34/0815)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Results from the MAN2 trials (NCT00120419, NCT00247494), whose status is unknown
- A controlled trial with virological and clinical endpoints, with a safety monitoring plan for opportunistic infections
- Route compatibility assessment, which is still pending

Among the other predictions, only multiple sclerosis also reached L3. There, one small early-phase trial tests MMF directly, as an add-on to interferon beta-1a (NCT00618527). The remaining predictions (ranks 2–5 and 7–10) have no clinical evidence retrieved and are best treated as graph artefacts or preclinical-only leads.
