## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. The following is based on general pharmacology of MMF. It inhibits inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase (IMPDH) and depletes guanosine nucleotide pools. This suppresses lymphocyte proliferation, which may reduce the pool of activated CD4+ target cells for HIV.

Depleting guanosine nucleotides may also lower intracellular dGTP and enhance the activity of nucleoside analogue reverse transcriptase inhibitors such as abacavir and didanosine. Small studies suggest the combination with antiretroviral therapy is tolerated. However, there is no convincing evidence of a durable virological benefit, and the immunosuppressive effect could in theory compromise immune reconstitution.

The original indication list is also missing from the data, so the link between the original and new indications cannot be assessed directly. The prediction should be regarded as a hypothesis.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic rationale (IMPDH inhibition and potentiation of nucleoside analogues) is plausible. However, the trials found are withdrawn, indirect, or unrelated to HIV, and the older studies show no convincing durable virological benefit. The registration and safety data are also incomplete, and immunosuppression in HIV carries a theoretical risk. The data gaps flagged in the compiled data also prevent safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The SAHPRA Professional Information (warnings, contraindications and approved indications)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Full review of the 2004 RCT (PMID 15353978) and the other MMF-HIV studies to quantify virological and immunological outcomes
- A DDI assessment against South African first-line antiretroviral regimens
- A risk-benefit analysis of immunosuppression in HIV, with particular attention to opportunistic infections (including tuberculosis) and cytopenias

**Other predictions in the data:** Multiple sclerosis (score 99.54%, L2) has the strongest supporting evidence of the other candidates, with small Phase 2 trials, a Cochrane review of MMF, and a retrospective study of 344 patients. It may merit its own evaluation. Hemosiderosis (L4) rests on case-level literature only. All remaining predictions are model output only (L5) and should be held.
