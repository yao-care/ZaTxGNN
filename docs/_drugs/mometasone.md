# Mometasone: From Allergic and Inflammatory Conditions to Primary Cutaneous T-Cell Lymphoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Mometasone is a potent corticosteroid, marketed in South Africa as a topical cream and as nasal sprays. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **primary cutaneous T-cell lymphoma**. Currently there are **no registered clinical trials** and only **2 case reports**, both indirectly relevant, so this remains a model prediction with little supporting evidence.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Mometasone is a synthetic glucocorticoid that acts on the glucocorticoid receptor (NR3C1). It is used intranasally for asthma, allergic rhinitis and nasal polyposis, and topically for allergic skin disorders.

Topical corticosteroids are an established skin-directed therapy for early-stage mycosis fungoides (patch/plaque-stage cutaneous T-cell lymphoma). They act through glucocorticoid receptor-mediated anti-inflammatory and pro-apoptotic effects on skin-infiltrating T cells. This gives a plausible mechanistic link, and the high TxGNN score is consistent with it.

However, no mometasone-specific clinical data are available. The two retrieved papers are only indirectly relevant: one is a pseudolymphoma case treated with tapinarof, and the other is a paediatric mycosis fungoides case report. The Evidence Level of L4 reflects mechanistic plausibility only, not demonstrated efficacy.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic rationale (topical corticosteroids in early-stage mycosis fungoides) is plausible, but there are no trials and no mometasone-specific literature. The only publications are two indirectly relevant case reports, so the evidence is insufficient to support progression. The other nine predicted indications (for example Crohn's colitis and myelodysplastic syndrome) have model predictions only (L5) and no identified mechanism or evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mometasone-specific clinical data in early-stage mycosis fungoides or cutaneous T-cell lymphoma (trials, series or comparative studies against other topical corticosteroids)
- Safety information from the SAHPRA Professional Information (warnings and contraindications), which is required before safety screening
- Approved indication text for the three SAHPRA registrations
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Assessment of route compatibility, since only the topical cream is relevant to skin lymphoma
