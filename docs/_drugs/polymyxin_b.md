# Polymyxin B: From Topical Antibacterial Use to Bronchitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Polymyxin B is a polypeptide antibiotic active against Gram-negative bacteria. The registration data compiled for this report do not state its approved indications. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **bronchitis**, but there are **no registered clinical trials** and only **12 publications**, most of them old, indirect or animal studies. Several publications also describe **bronchoconstriction after inhaled polymyxin B**, which is a safety signal.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Polymyxin B is a bactericidal antibiotic that binds lipid A and disrupts the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria. It could therefore plausibly act against bacterial bronchitis or tracheobronchitis caused by multidrug-resistant Gram-negative organisms such as *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and *Acinetobacter baumannii*.

Two small observational studies support this idea. One is a comparison with other antimicrobials in ventilator-associated pneumonia and tracheobronchitis (2013). The other is a series of 19 patients treated with inhaled polymyxin B for multidrug-resistant Gram-negative respiratory infections (2007). Both concern hospital-acquired, resistant infections, not common bronchitis, which is mostly viral.

The wider literature on "polymyxin B and bronchitis" is dominated by a different theme. Polymyxin B was used as a **bronchial provocation agent** in asthma and chronic obstructive bronchitis, and it is also used to induce eosinophilic bronchitis in animal models. These papers show that inhaled polymyxin B can provoke airway reactions, so the prediction is mechanistically plausible only for a narrow group of resistant Gram-negative infections.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

Currently no related clinical trials registered for bronchitis.

*Note:* For the broader prediction "respiratory tract infectious disease", trials exist, mostly in ventilator-associated pneumonia. Examples are [NCT07086391](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07086391) (nebulisation methods, recruiting) and [NCT06076603](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06076603) (lung fluid pharmacokinetics). These do not directly evaluate bronchitis.

## Safety Considerations

- **Bronchoconstriction with inhalation:** Published reports describe bronchospasm and bronchial hyperreactivity after inhaled polymyxin B, especially in people with asthma or chronic obstructive bronchitis (PMIDs 4322737, 231152). Mast cell and basophil degranulation with histamine release is the proposed mechanism.
- **Drug interactions:** 73 interactions were found in the interaction database. Those graded **Major** include:
  - Amikacin
  - Cisatracurium
  - Cidofovir
  - Deferasirox
  - Botulinum toxin type A
  - Botulinum neurotoxin A/B immune globulin
  - Intravenous immunoglobulin G
  - Human cytomegalovirus immune globulin
  - Iodinated contrast agents (iothalamic acid, diatrizoate)
  - Live oral cholera vaccine

  Examples graded **Moderate** include oestrogens (ethinylestradiol, estradiol, mestranol), mesalazine, balsalazide, ibandronate, capreomycin, clofarabine and picosulfuric acid.

Warnings and contraindications from the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) were not available. Please consult the PI before any use, and report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The prediction is supported only by a high model score, two small observational studies in resistant ventilator-associated respiratory infections, and no registered bronchitis trials.
- Inhaled polymyxin B has a documented bronchoconstriction risk in people with airway disease.
- Common bronchitis is largely viral, so there is little rationale for an antibiotic of last resort.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SAHPRA PI warnings and contraindications, and the approved indications of the registered products
- Mechanism-of-action data
- A narrower, clinically defined target such as multidrug-resistant Gram-negative tracheobronchitis or ventilator-associated respiratory infection, instead of general bronchitis
- Prospective safety and efficacy data for inhaled or nebulised dosing, including bronchospasm monitoring and nephrotoxicity or neurotoxicity with intravenous use
- Antimicrobial stewardship review, since polymyxin B is a last-line agent

*For context:* The predicted indication **conjunctivitis** has randomised trial support for topical polymyxin B/trimethoprim combinations (for example PMIDs 19043943, 19043945, 23092529). This is largely an established use of the topical products rather than true repurposing, and it fits the registered topical and eye formulations better than bronchitis does.
