## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, minocycline is a member of the tetracycline class of antibiotics. Its efficacy in bacterial infections and acne is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to inflammatory conditions of the ocular surface.

A plausible rationale is that tetracyclines have anti-inflammatory activity and can inhibit matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), enzymes involved in tissue breakdown. These properties could in theory help calm inflammation and limit damage to the corneal surface. This reasoning is speculative: no trials or publications were found in the data compiled for this report to support it, and the high TxGNN score reflects a model prediction only.

Other top-ranked predictions show the same pattern. Exposure keratitis (99.20%) and neurotrophic keratopathy (98.98%) are also eye-surface conditions with no supporting evidence. Of the ten predictions reviewed, only two have any evidence attached, and in neither case is it direct:

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-level cautions (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: Minocycline is linked to contraindication or caution in kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, *Clostridium difficile* colitis, pseudotumor cerebri/intracranial hypertension, perinatal necrotizing enterocolitis, pyoureter and oesophageal dyskinesia. These should be checked against the approved label.
- **Drug Interactions** (100 interactions recorded; the first 20 reviewed):
  - **Major**: isotretinoin and acitretin. Combining tetracyclines with retinoids is associated with raised intracranial pressure.
  - **Moderate**: ethinylestradiol, amoxicillin, ampicillin, calcium phosphate, calcium acetate, sodium citrate, bismuth subcitrate potassium, balsalazide, cannabidiol, brentuximab vedotin, topical aminolevulinic acid, and asparaginase products.
  - **Minor**: acetazolamide, hydrochlorothiazide, amiloride and bumetanide.

Warnings and contraindications from the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) were not available. Please refer to the PI for full safety information. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.
