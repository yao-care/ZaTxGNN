## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this drug. Clobetasol is a topical glucocorticoid. As a general assumption not backed by the supplied data, a potent glucocorticoid could act on skin-homing malignant T cells through glucocorticoid-receptor anti-inflammatory and pro-apoptotic effects.

The link between the drug's usual use and the predicted indication is skin-localised disease. Early-stage cutaneous T-cell lymphoma (mycosis fungoides) is a lymphoma confined to the skin. Its patches and plaques can be treated with topical agents applied directly to the lesions.

The papers retrieved for mycosis fungoides were filed under the label "lymphosarcoma", not "primary cutaneous T-cell lymphoma". These two labels should be reconciled before any decision is made. The plausible mechanism is local, so it would not extend to nodal or systemic lymphoma.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for safety information. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

The retrieved literature raises two points that need review:
- **Local skin effects**: A 2020 observational study in early-stage mycosis fungoides examined skin adverse effects of prolonged clobetasol 0.05% cream (PMID 32603400).
- **Systemic effects**: A case report describes topical clobetasol as an overlooked cause of Cushing syndrome (PMID 33902425). Prolonged use over large areas of skin, as chronic lymphoma treatment would involve, needs careful monitoring.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The model score is very high, but no trials or literature were retrieved under the label "primary cutaneous T-cell lymphoma". The supporting evidence (provisional L3) is observational and sits under a different label. No Phase 3 RCT of clobetasol for lymphoma was found. The plausible mechanism applies only to early-stage skin disease.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Reconcile the labels "primary cutaneous T-cell lymphoma" and "lymphosarcoma", and narrow the indication to early-stage mycosis fungoides.
- Verify study designs and grade the full set of 20 papers, since only 10 were supplied.
- Obtain the SAHPRA package insert for both registrations, covering approved indications, warnings and contraindications.
- Obtain mechanism of action data from DrugBank.
- Define a monitoring plan for prolonged use, covering local skin atrophy and systemic corticosteroid effects.
