# Clobetasol: From Topical Corticosteroid-Responsive Skin Disease to Primary Cutaneous T-Cell Lymphoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Clobetasol is a super-potent topical corticosteroid that is currently registered in South Africa as a cream and an ointment.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **primary cutaneous T-cell lymphoma** (mainly early-stage mycosis fungoides).
No registered clinical trials were found for this indication, but **16 publications** support the direction, including several cohort studies.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Clobetasol is a super-potent topical glucocorticoid. Glucocorticoid receptor activation is known to induce apoptosis of malignant T cells and to suppress local inflammatory and cytokine signalling in skin lesions.

This is a plausible mechanism for early-stage (patch or plaque) mycosis fungoides, the most common form of cutaneous T-cell lymphoma. Topical corticosteroids are already used in clinical practice for this setting.

Note that the prediction applies to skin-limited disease. It should not be extended to systemic or advanced lymphoma.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

Currently no related clinical trials registered for primary cutaneous T-cell lymphoma.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screen returned 100 entries, all with an unclassified severity level. Examples include other corticosteroids (prednisone, prednisolone, hydrocortisone, triamcinolone), calcitriol, and pantoprazole. The severity and clinical significance of these entries have not been confirmed, so they should be checked against the PI and a clinical interaction reference.
- **Literature-based safety signals**: Prolonged or extensive use of potent topical steroids has been associated with skin adverse effects (reported in the MF cohort study) and with iatrogenic Cushing syndrome and HPA axis suppression (case reports, including children). Because MF is a chronic disease needing prolonged treatment, cumulative exposure needs monitoring.

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Cohort-level evidence, including a roughly 200-patient experience with response rates above 90%, supports topical clobetasol in early-stage mycosis fungoides, and the drug is already marketed in South Africa. The evidence is observational and no registered trials exist, so use should stay limited to skin-restricted, early-stage disease under specialist supervision.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the SAHPRA-approved PI for warnings and contraindications, which were not available in the data compiled for this report
- Confirmation of the approved indications for Dermovate and Xenovate
- Histological confirmation of diagnosis and staging by a dermatologist or haemato-oncologist before treatment, since MF is often misdiagnosed as psoriasis or eczema
- A monitoring plan for skin atrophy and systemic steroid absorption (including HPA axis effects) during long-term use
- Prospective or comparative studies, ideally a randomised trial, to strengthen the evidence
