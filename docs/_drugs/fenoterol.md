## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

ChEMBL (EMBL-EBI) records the mechanism of action of fenoterol as: Beta-2 adrenergic receptor agonist ([ChEMBL CHEMBL32800 (fenoterol): drug mechanism](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl/explore/compound/CHEMBL32800), CC BY-SA 3.0; accessed 2026-11-19.) Fenoterol is known as a beta-2 adrenergic agonist, and pharmacology records show activity at beta-1, beta-2 and beta-3 adrenoceptors. It has been described as a bronchodilator and as an agent used to suppress premature labour.

A theoretical link to multiple system atrophy (MSA), which involves autonomic failure and orthostatic hypotension, is conceivable through cardiovascular effects. However, beta-2 mediated vasodilation would more likely worsen orthostatic hypotension than relieve it. The prediction therefore rests on the model score alone and is mechanistically questionable.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions / Pharmacology**: Fenoterol acts on beta-1, beta-2 and beta-3 adrenoceptors. No drug–drug interaction records with named co-medications were available.
- **Mechanism-related concerns for the predicted use**: Beta-2 agonism can cause tachycardia, tremor, peripheral vasodilation and hypokalaemia. In a condition characterised by orthostatic hypotension, vasodilation could be harmful.

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction for multiple system atrophy has no supporting clinical trials or publications (evidence level L5), and the known pharmacology of beta-2 agonists suggests possible harm rather than benefit in orthostatic hypotension.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism-of-action data and a plausible biological rationale linking beta-2 agonism to multiple system atrophy
- Preclinical or clinical studies that directly address this disease
- SAHPRA Professional Information review for warnings and contraindications
- A cardiovascular and autonomic safety assessment in patients with autonomic failure
