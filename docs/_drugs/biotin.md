## One-Sentence Summary

Biotin is a B-vitamin that acts as a cofactor for carboxylase enzymes involved in fatty acid, glucose and amino acid metabolism.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be relevant for **dyspepsia**, with a score of **0.9943**.
There are currently **no registered clinical trials** and **no publication that directly studies biotin for dyspepsia**, so this remains a model prediction only.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available in the data compiled for this report. Biotin is generally understood as a cofactor for carboxylase enzymes that take part in fatty acid, glucose and amino acid metabolism.

The TxGNN score is very high, but no direct mechanism linking biotin to dyspepsia is evident. The retrieved literature covers biotin deficiency in an infant on amino acid formula, enteroendocrine cells in irritable bowel syndrome, gut microbiota disturbance, and *H. pylori* gastritis. None of these studies tests biotin as a treatment for dyspepsia. Any link would be indirect, for example through biotin production by gut microbiota, and is speculative at this stage.
