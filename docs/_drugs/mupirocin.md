# Mupirocin: From Topical Antibacterial Use to Pleural Empyema

## One-Sentence Summary

Mupirocin is a topical antibacterial that acts against *Staphylococcus aureus*. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **pleural empyema**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** currently support this prediction. It rests on a computational score alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Mupirocin inhibits bacterial isoleucyl-tRNA synthetase and is active against *Staphylococcus aureus*. This organism can cause empyema, which is the only mechanistic bridge to the predicted indication. The evidence pack does not include a formal mechanism-of-action entry, so this description comes from the candidate's rationale notes.

The bridge is weak. Mupirocin is a topical agent with no established systemic or intrapleural use. Empyema is a deep-seated pleural infection, so drug penetration, formulation and route would all be unresolved problems. The high TxGNN score reflects a computational association, not clinical support. No route compatibility assessment has been completed.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Pleural empyema is supported only by a model score (L5), with no trials or literature. The topical-only formulation also does not fit a deep pleural infection.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Obtain the SAHPRA package insert and extract its warnings and contraindications. This gap blocks safety screening.
- Complete the route compatibility assessment. A pleural indication would need a non-topical route or formulation, which is not registered in South Africa.
- Retrieve mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank.
- Search for direct clinical or preclinical evidence in pleural infection.

**Note on other predictions for this drug:** Staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome has more supporting evidence (L3, "Research Question"). This consists mainly of case reports, epidemiology studies and one 2023 study of IV antibiotics combined with 2% mupirocin ointment. It has no RCTs, and reports of mupirocin-resistant *S. aureus* clones are a stewardship concern. It may be a better candidate for follow-up than pleural empyema.
