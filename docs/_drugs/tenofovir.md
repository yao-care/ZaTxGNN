## Tenofovir: From Chronic Hepatitis B and HIV Infection to Feline Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Tenofovir is a nucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitor, originally used to treat chronic hepatitis B virus infection, HIV infection and AIDS.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **feline acquired immunodeficiency syndrome**, a veterinary condition.
Support is limited to **2 publications** (animal studies) and **no registered clinical trials**, so this is essentially a species extension of the existing HIV indication rather than a new human use.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, tenofovir is a nucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitor with established use in HIV and hepatitis B.

Feline immunodeficiency virus (FIV) is a lentivirus, and its reverse transcriptase is sensitive to tenofovir (PMPA). The mechanistic rationale is therefore plausible. However, this is a veterinary indication. It extends the existing HIV indication to another species and offers little new value for human drug repurposing.

The two other predictions in the data are weaker or equally non-human:
- **Simian immunodeficiency virus infection** (score 99.96%): extensive macaque studies show protection against SHIV acquisition with tenofovir-based regimens (oral, vaginal gel, rectal douche, implants). These are preclinical models of HIV and support the existing HIV treatment and PrEP indications, not a distinct human indication.
- **A rare neurodevelopmental disorder with ataxic gait, absent speech and decreased cortical white matter** (score 99.96%): no mechanistic link, trials or literature. This is likely a knowledge-graph artefact.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The predicted indication is veterinary and is a species extension of the existing human HIV indication. Evidence consists of animal studies only (L4), with no registered trials. It adds little value as a human repurposing candidate, and the SIV and neurodevelopmental predictions do not change this.
