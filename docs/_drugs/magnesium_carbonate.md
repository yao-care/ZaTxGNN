# Magnesium Carbonate: From Peptic Ulcer Disease to Active Peptic Ulcer Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Magnesium carbonate is an antacid, originally used for peptic ulcer disease, reflux disease, oesophagitis, constipation and dyspepsia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **active peptic ulcer disease**, but this is largely a restatement of its existing use rather than true repurposing.
Currently there are **0 clinical trials** and **1 publication** (an in vitro antacid study) supporting this prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacology, magnesium carbonate is an antacid that neutralises gastric acid. This is relevant to acid-related conditions of the stomach and duodenum.

Active peptic ulcer disease overlaps closely with the drug's existing peptic ulcer indication. The prediction therefore reflects the drug's known use rather than a genuinely new therapeutic area. The same is true of gastric ulcer and duodenal ulcer, which are also predicted.

Other predictions are weaker. Peptic ulcer perforation, duodenal obstruction and gastroparesis have no plausible antacid mechanism. Perforation and obstruction need surgical or procedural management, and gastroparesis is a motility disorder. Their high scores likely reflect proximity to peptic ulcer in the knowledge graph.

Modern ulcer healing relies mainly on proton pump inhibitors and *H. pylori* eradication. Antacids are now mostly used for symptom relief.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindication signals from the knowledge graph (not label text):** The drug-disease contraindication links in the TxGNN knowledge graph include:
- Kidney disease, nephrocalcinosis, hypercalcaemia, hyperparathyroidism and phosphorus metabolism disease
- Liver failure, liver disease, hepatic encephalopathy and hepatic coma
- Porphyria
- Thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis and anaemia
- Diarrhoeal disease
- Sarcoidosis, sleep apnoea syndromes, anxiety and related psychiatric conditions, and substance abuse/dependence

These are not verified against the SAHPRA-approved label. Many are likely to be class or combination-product signals and should be interpreted with caution.

**Drug interactions** (DDInter, 100 interactions recorded; selected examples):
- **Major:** Dolutegravir
- **Moderate:** Doxycycline, alendronic acid, risedronic acid, acetylsalicylic acid, atazanavir, azithromycin, cholecalciferol, acalabrutinib, atropine, amphetamine, baloxavir marboxil, benzphetamine
- **Minor:** Famotidine, ranitidine, alprazolam, atenolol, acebutolol, axitinib, benazepril

Interactions with dolutegravir are particularly relevant in South Africa given the large HIV treatment programme. Antacids containing polyvalent cations can reduce absorption of integrase inhibitors, so dosing needs to be separated. Confirm timing against the PI.

Label warnings and contraindications from the SAHPRA package insert are not available. Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for safety information. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The prediction for active peptic ulcer disease largely restates the existing antacid use. The only supporting publication is an in vitro study, and there are no registered trials. No other predicted indication has stronger evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The SAHPRA package insert (Reg. No. 45/24/0919), to confirm the registered indication, warnings and contraindications. This is a blocking gap for safety screening.
- Mechanism of action data, for example from DrugBank.
- Clinical evidence in ulcer patients comparing magnesium carbonate with current standard care. Otherwise, treat the prediction as confirming the existing indication rather than a new one.
- A review of the interaction risks, especially with dolutegravir, doxycycline, bisphosphonates and other cation-sensitive drugs, and of renal impairment precautions.
