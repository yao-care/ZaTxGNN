# Leucine: From Nutritional Amino Acid Component to Congenital Prothrombin Deficiency

## One-Sentence Summary

Leucine is an essential branched-chain amino acid. In South Africa it is registered only as an ingredient of parenteral nutrition and peritoneal dialysis solutions.
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is **congenital prothrombin deficiency** (score 99.0%), but **no clinical trials and no publications** support it.
The better-supported candidate in the data is **postmenopausal osteoporosis**, which has **1 clinical trial** and indirect literature.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, leucine is a component of amino acid mixtures used in parenteral nutrition, and it has no established role in coagulation.

For the top-ranked prediction, **congenital prothrombin deficiency**, no mechanistic link is supported. This is a monogenic coagulation factor disorder (F2 gene), and leucine has no known role in prothrombin synthesis or function. The high score is a model output only.

Among the other top-10 predictions, two have indirect biological rationale:

- **Postmenopausal osteoporosis (rank 6, L4):** Leucine supports muscle protein synthesis through mTORC1 and may influence bone through muscle-bone crosstalk. Metabolomic studies associate amino acid metabolism with bone density in postmenopausal women. These findings are associative, not interventional.
- **Acne (rank 3, L4):** Leucine activates mTORC1, and Western-diet-driven mTORC1 signalling is implicated in acne. This suggests leucine could **aggravate** rather than treat acne, so the direction of effect may be opposite to a therapeutic use.

The remaining predictions (gastroparesis, bone Paget disease, vitamin D deficiency, sclerosing cholangitis, pregnancy-associated osteoporosis, albinism-deafness syndrome, Worth syndrome) have no supportive mechanism or evidence in the data. Many literature hits are keyword matches on "leucine" (for example leucine-rich proteins and the leucine aminopeptidase enzyme) rather than studies of leucine as a treatment.

## Safety Considerations

No label-based warnings or contraindications are available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for safety information. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

The TxGNN knowledge graph lists diseases linked to leucine through contraindication relationships. These are model-derived, not from label text, and should be verified:

- **Renal:** kidney disease, chronic kidney disease, chronic renal failure syndrome, end stage renal failure, nephrolithiasis, nephrocalcinosis, acute urate nephropathy
- **Cardiovascular:** heart disease, cardiovascular disease, ventricular fibrillation, digitalis poisoning
- **Metabolic:** phenylketonuria, disorder of phenylalanine metabolism, phosphorus metabolism disease, hypercalcemia disease, humoral hypercalcemia of malignancy, sarcoidosis
- **Other:** pyoureter, nephrolithiasis susceptibility caused by SLC26A1

Additional points:
- Safety of leucine supplementation in pregnancy is unresolved, which is relevant to the pregnancy-associated osteoporosis prediction.
- For acne, mTORC1 activation raises the possibility of worsening the condition.
- No drug interaction data were found.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top prediction, congenital prothrombin deficiency, rests on a model score alone, with no trials, no literature and no plausible mechanism (L5). Postmenopausal osteoporosis has the most relevant signal (L4: one small supplementation trial and associative metabolomic data), but this is not efficacy evidence and the recommendation there is limited to a research question. The data compiled for this report also leave the SAHPRA label safety information outstanding.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SAHPRA package insert warnings and contraindications (blocking gap)
- Mechanism of action data for leucine
- Published outcomes of NCT01757340, especially bone mineral density results
- A preclinical or early-phase study of leucine in a bone or muscle-bone endpoint, if postmenopausal osteoporosis is pursued
- Review of the knowledge-graph contraindications (renal, cardiovascular, phenylketonuria) against the PI
- Verification of the registration data, including the ipratropium entry and the missing indication text
