# Ramipril: From Hypertension to Pulmonary Hypertension (Unclear Multifactorial Mechanism)

## One-Sentence Summary

Ramipril is an ACE inhibitor marketed in South Africa as several oral products (the records supplied do not state an approved indication). The TxGNN model ranks **pulmonary hypertension with unclear multifactorial mechanism** as its top prediction, but **no clinical trials and no relevant publications** support this specific prediction. The strongest evidence in this pack is for a different, lower-ranked candidate, **cerebral artery occlusion**, and it is still only indirect (1 Phase 2 trial, mostly rodent data).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the supplied pack. Ramipril is an angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor, and ACE inhibition could plausibly affect vascular remodelling. That is the only mechanistic link offered for the lead prediction.

The label "pulmonary hypertension with unclear multifactorial mechanism" is non-specific, so the mechanistic argument is weak. The 20 papers retrieved for the related prediction, "pulmonary hypertension owing to lung disease and/or hypoxia", are about hypoxia biology in neurological disease, cancer and fibrosis. None involve ramipril or pulmonary hypertension, so they look like keyword matches rather than drug-specific evidence.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for safety information. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

One point from the prediction notes: ACE inhibitors are known to carry renal risk in renovascular disease (for example, bilateral renal artery stenosis). This needs careful review before any use in the renovascular hypertension candidates.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The lead prediction is supported only by a model score (99.93%), with no trials and no relevant literature, and the disease label is non-specific. Ramipril is widely marketed in South Africa, but there is no evidence to justify pursuing this indication now.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SAHPRA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for any safety screening).
- Mechanism of action data, for example from DrugBank.
- A more specific pulmonary hypertension classification, then a targeted search for ramipril-specific human evidence.
- If a fallback candidate is wanted, cerebral artery occlusion is the best-supported (L4). It would need a ramipril-specific clinical trial with stroke outcomes, since the current data are surrogate endpoints and rodent studies, some of which involved other agents or showed blood pressure–dependent effects.
