# Roxithromycin: From Macrolide Antibacterial to Leprosy

## One-Sentence Summary

Roxithromycin is a macrolide antibiotic that is registered and marketed in South Africa as an oral tablet.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data for roxithromycin is not available in the evidence pack. Macrolides as a class inhibit bacterial protein synthesis by binding the 50S ribosomal subunit. Leprosy is caused by *Mycobacterium leprae*, so an antibacterial link is plausible.

The supplied literature supports this link only at the class level. In a mouse footpad study, roxithromycin and clarithromycin were both consistently active and bactericidal against *M. leprae*, while erythromycin and azithromycin were inactive. Clarithromycin was the stronger of the two, possibly because it reaches higher levels at the site of infection. A Japanese review also notes anti-*M. leprae* activity for roxithromycin, plus anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects that may matter in leprous neuropathy.

Because the mouse data show that activity differs between macrolides, results cannot simply be carried over from one macrolide to another. No human data were supplied.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for safety information. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

The interaction query in the evidence pack returned no results, which does not mean there are no interactions. One in vitro study (PMID 37971309) found that macrolide and ketolide antibiotics reduced the cytotoxic effect of trastuzumab emtansine in HER2-positive breast cancer cells. This is a preclinical signal only, but it should be checked if roxithromycin is ever considered in patients receiving that therapy.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The leprosy prediction is biologically plausible and has some in vitro and mouse support. However, there are no human trials, the evidence is class-level, and clarithromycin outperformed roxithromycin in the animal model. The other nine predicted indications in the pack are also on Hold, with no trials and little or no relevant literature.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The SAHPRA package insert, including warnings, contraindications and the approved indication (currently a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Roxithromycin-specific efficacy data against *M. leprae*, and a comparison with clarithromycin and current standard leprosy regimens
- Any human clinical data, including trial registrations in ClinicalTrials.gov, SANCTR or PACTR
- A drug interaction check once the package insert is available
