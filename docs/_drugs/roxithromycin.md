## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Roxithromycin is a macrolide antibiotic that inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by binding to the 50S ribosomal subunit. Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Its in vitro antibacterial activity is similar to erythromycin, mainly against Gram-positive bacteria and some Gram-negative bacteria such as *Legionella pneumophila*.

Leprosy is caused by *Mycobacterium leprae*, a bacterial infection, so an antibacterial rationale is plausible. In a mouse footpad study (1991), roxithromycin and clarithromycin were consistently active and bactericidal against *M. leprae*, whereas erythromycin and azithromycin were inactive. Clarithromycin was superior to roxithromycin, partly because of higher drug levels at the infection site. Laboratory work from 1988 and 1989 also tested macrolides against *M. leprae*. A Japanese review (1999) notes anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory activity for roxithromycin and clarithromycin, which may matter for leprosy nerve inflammation.

This support is preclinical. No roxithromycin-specific human trial data were found, and clarithromycin is the better-characterised macrolide for this use.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screen returned one pharmacology-based entry, roxithromycin acting at the motilin receptor (MLNR). This is a target-binding property, not a clinical drug-drug interaction.
- **Potential antagonism with antibody-drug conjugates**: A 2023 in vitro study (PMID 37971309) found that macrolide and ketolide antibiotics inhibited the cytotoxic effect of trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1) in HER2-positive breast cancer cells. Clinical relevance is unconfirmed, but caution is advised if roxithromycin is considered alongside T-DM1.

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for warnings and contraindications. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (treat as a research question)**

**Rationale:**
Leprosy has a plausible antibacterial rationale, and mouse studies show roxithromycin is active against *M. leprae*. However, the evidence is entirely preclinical or review-level, there are no human trials, and clarithromycin appears more active. SAHPRA safety information is also missing, so safety screening cannot proceed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**

- Mechanism of action data
- Comparative review against clarithromycin and current WHO leprosy regimens
- Clinical data, or a proposed trial, in leprosy patients
- Interaction check against standard multidrug leprosy therapy
- Confirmation of EML status and route compatibility (oral tablet)
