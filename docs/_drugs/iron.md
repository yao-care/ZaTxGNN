## Iron: From Iron Deficiency Anaemia to Plummer-Vinson Syndrome
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Iron is an essential mineral, originally used to treat iron deficiency and other nutritional and microcytic anaemias.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **Plummer-Vinson syndrome** (the top-scoring prediction with any supporting literature), with **no registered clinical trials** and **18 publications** (mostly reviews and case reports) currently supporting this direction.
This is better viewed as an extension of the existing anaemia use than as true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Iron is the essential component of haemoglobin, and replacing it corrects the underlying deficiency in iron deficiency anaemia.

Plummer-Vinson syndrome is defined by a triad: iron deficiency anaemia, dysphagia and upper oesophageal webs. Iron repletion is the established first-line management, and the literature reports that dysphagia often resolves with iron supplementation, sometimes before the blood counts normalise. The link is therefore biologically coherent, because treating the iron deficiency treats the syndrome's core driver.

Because the syndrome contains iron deficiency anaemia, which is already an original indication, this is an extension of the approved use rather than a new mechanism. Some patients still need oesophageal dilatation, and the syndrome carries an increased risk of postcricoid and oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma, so long-term surveillance is recommended.

Other predictions were reviewed and are weak:
- **Vitamin B12- and folate-independent constitutional megaloblastic anaemia**: no evidence. Iron is not a recognised treatment for megaloblastic anaemia, and the high score likely reflects proximity to other anaemias in the knowledge graph.
- **Non-syndromic oesophageal malformation**: no evidence. Structural malformations are not corrected by iron.
- **Biotin metabolic disease**: no trials. The literature is off-topic, and these disorders are treated with biotin.
- **Vitamin deficiency disorder** (L3): trials mostly test iron in combination with vitamins in iron-deficient populations, so they do not show that iron treats vitamin deficiency itself.
- **Oesophageal disease** (L4): one small pilot trial of IV iron in anaemia associated with oesophagogastric cancer. The link is indirect, and iron's role in ferroptosis may even imply a risk in oesophageal malignancy.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph** (these are not from label text and must be verified against the SAHPRA Professional Information): peptic ulcer disease, hereditary haemochromatosis, haemosiderosis, iron metabolism disease, Wilson disease, ulcerative colitis, inflammatory bowel disease, haemolytic anaemia and gastroenteritis.

**Drug interactions**: 79 interactions were identified. Examples:
- **Major**: dolutegravir and bictegravir. Iron can reduce integrase inhibitor absorption, which is particularly relevant in South Africa given the HIV treatment programme. Separate dosing or follow the label guidance.
- **Moderate**: tetracyclines (doxycycline, tetracycline), ciprofloxacin and delafloxacin, bisphosphonates (alendronic acid, risedronic acid, ibandronate), proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, rabeprazole), levodopa, cefdinir, chloramphenicol, deferiprone, magnesium oxide, sodium citrate, pancrelipase and baloxavir marboxil.
- **Minor**: cimetidine.

**Other safety points from the literature**: hypophosphataemia can follow some intravenous iron formulations (PMID 34534708). Iron's role in ferroptosis in oesophageal cancer is a theoretical consideration, since Plummer-Vinson syndrome already carries a malignancy risk.

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
- For Plummer-Vinson syndrome, iron repletion is already established first-line management, and the evidence (L4) is consistent but observational. It is an extension of the existing iron deficiency anaemia use rather than a new indication, so the risk is low, though no controlled data exist.
- The other predictions (megaloblastic anaemia, oesophageal malformation, biotin metabolic disease, vitamin deficiency disorder, oesophageal disease) should be held, as the evidence is absent, indirect or biologically implausible.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The SAHPRA Professional Information (warnings, contraindications and approved indications), which was a blocking gap in the compiled data
- Mechanism of action data
- Confirmation of treatment outcomes (dysphagia resolution, web regression) from the retrospective cohort (PMID 40802140) and other studies
- A surveillance plan, such as endoscopy for oesophageal and postcricoid carcinoma risk
- Screening for the contraindicated conditions listed above and review of interacting medicines, especially dolutegravir and bictegravir, before starting
- Selection of the oral or parenteral route and formulation based on tolerance, absorption and the patient's gastrointestinal condition
