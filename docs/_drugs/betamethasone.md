# Betamethasone: From Corticosteroid-Responsive Inflammatory Conditions to Alopecia Areata

## One-Sentence Summary

Betamethasone is a corticosteroid, marketed in South Africa as creams, ointments, injections, syrup and other forms. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **alopecia areata**, an autoimmune hair-loss disease. This direction is supported by **6 registered clinical trials** and **20 publications**, including one completed Phase 2 randomised trial of oral betamethasone mini-pulse therapy.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on general glucocorticoid pharmacology, betamethasone has anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects. Its efficacy in inflammatory and immune-mediated conditions is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to alopecia areata.

Alopecia areata is an autoimmune disease in which T-cells attack the hair follicle and cause non-scarring hair loss. A corticosteroid can dampen this immune attack. Topical corticosteroids are already established agents for this disorder, and betamethasone is used clinically as a topical, intralesional and oral mini-pulse treatment. In the trials below it appears as an active arm or as the comparator.

The evidence has limits. No Phase 3 RCT is in the data, and several trials use betamethasone only as a comparator. In two trials (NCT02350023 and NCT03535233) the corticosteroid is not confirmed to be betamethasone.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
One completed Phase 2 RCT and several published randomised studies include betamethasone (topical, intralesional or oral mini-pulse) in alopecia areata. However, there is no Phase 3 evidence and several trials use betamethasone only as a comparator, so the evidence supports cautious progression rather than an unconditional Go.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SAHPRA package insert warnings and contraindications (currently blocking, so S1 safety screening cannot proceed without them).
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank.
- Confirmation of the corticosteroid used in NCT02350023 and NCT03535233.
- Guardrails for use:
  - Limit treatment duration.
  - Monitor for skin atrophy and HPA-axis suppression with topical use.
  - Monitor systemic adverse effects with oral pulses.
  - Counsel patients that relapse is expected after stopping.

**Other predicted indications:**
All other predictions are on Hold and the evidence for them is weak or absent. For alopecia mucinosa, telogen effluvium, folliculitis decalvans and idiopathic steroid-sensitive nephrotic syndrome, the literature is limited to case reports, indirect studies or a single 1981 report. The remaining predictions rest on the model score alone. Sporadic idiopathic steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome is contradictory by definition, because the condition does not respond to corticosteroids.
