# Betamethasone: From Autoimmune and Inflammatory Conditions to Alopecia Areata

## One-Sentence Summary

Betamethasone is a potent glucocorticoid, originally used for inflammatory and autoimmune conditions such as Crohn disease, adrenocortical insufficiency, eosinophilic pneumonia and acute lymphoblastic leukaemia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **alopecia areata**,
with **6 registered clinical trials** (2 completed trials directly involving betamethasone) and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, betamethasone is a potent glucocorticoid with anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects.

Alopecia areata is an autoimmune condition in which the immune system attacks hair follicles (loss of follicular immune privilege, with CD8+ T-cell infiltration). Betamethasone is already used for other autoimmune and inflammatory diseases, so suppressing this immune attack is a mechanistically coherent extension. Topical, intralesional and oral mini-pulse regimens are all used in practice.

The high TxGNN score is consistent with this known mechanism. The same data also list lower-ranked predictions (alopecia mucinosa, telogen effluvium, folliculitis decalvans, and idiopathic steroid-sensitive nephrotic syndrome). These rest on case-level or very old evidence and are not recommended for advancement at present.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph** (these are drug-disease associations, not label text) include: hypertension, congestive heart failure, hyperglycaemia, epilepsy, herpes simplex virus keratitis, measles, urea cycle disorders (carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I deficiency, hyperargininemia, citrullinemia), and pituitary hormone deficiency conditions (including hypopituitarism and growth hormone deficiency).

**Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were identified. Examples include:
- **Major:** adalimumab
- **Moderate:** zidovudine, isotretinoin, acarbose, acebutolol, acetazolamide, ketorolac, ibuprofen, nifedipine, ethinylestradiol, fluvoxamine, fosamprenavir, aldesleukin, alemtuzumab, and tetanus toxoid vaccine
- **Minor:** formoterol, salbutamol

**Practical guardrails:** monitor for skin atrophy with topical use; with systemic pulses, watch for HPA-axis suppression and metabolic effects (glucose, blood pressure). Relapse after withdrawal is common.

SAHPRA package insert warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for full safety information. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Alopecia areata is supported by a completed Phase 2 trial with a betamethasone arm, a completed Phase 4 comparator trial, and several small randomised trials of topical, intralesional and oral mini-pulse regimens, consistent with a well-understood glucocorticoid mechanism. Most trials are small, use betamethasone as a comparator, and are single-region, so evidence is moderate (L2) rather than definitive. The other predicted indications are not recommended for advancement (Hold), except steroid-sensitive nephrotic syndrome, which remains a research question because prednisone or prednisolone is the established standard.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SAHPRA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Confirmation of the approved indications for the registered products, and whether alopecia areata is covered or would be off-label
- Selection of route and regimen (topical, intralesional or oral mini-pulse) with a monitoring plan for skin atrophy, HPA-axis suppression and metabolic effects
- Larger, placebo-controlled or head-to-head trials with relapse follow-up, ideally including African populations
