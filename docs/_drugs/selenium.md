## Selenium: From Parenteral Nutrition Component to Sclerosing Cholangitis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Selenium is an essential trace element. In South Africa it is registered only in parenteral nutrition (TPN) products, and the data compiled for this report give no approved indication text for it.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **sclerosing cholangitis**, but there are **0 registered clinical trials** and only **5 publications**, none of which tests selenium as a treatment.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Selenium is a known cofactor for glutathione peroxidases and other selenoproteins. These enzymes counter oxidative stress and inflammation, both of which are thought to contribute to cholestatic liver injury.

The literature offers only indirect support. One study of 32 patients with primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC) examined hepatic copper and selenium retention. A dietary survey described the eating habits of people with PSC. A murine proteomic study looked at fibrosis and sclerosing cholangitis. A case report described parenteral nutrition in a patient with PSC and ulcerative colitis. None of these tested selenium as a therapy.

The very high TxGNN score is therefore a computational signal only. It is not backed by clinical evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a high model score and indirect observational and preclinical literature. No trial or study tests selenium as a treatment for sclerosing cholangitis, and the package insert safety data are missing.


**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The SAHPRA package insert, to obtain warnings, contraindications and the approved indications.
- Detailed mechanism of action data for selenium.
- Interventional evidence, ideally a controlled trial, of selenium in sclerosing cholangitis.
- A dosing and safety plan that respects selenium's narrow therapeutic window.
- A review of the interaction with dimercaprol.
