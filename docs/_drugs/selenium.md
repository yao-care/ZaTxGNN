# Selenium: From Parenteral Nutrition Trace Element Use to Sclerosing Cholangitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Selenium is an essential trace element, and in South Africa it is registered in parenteral nutrition (TPN) type products. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **sclerosing cholangitis** (score 99.04%), but there are **0 clinical trials** and only **5 publications**, none of which tests selenium as a treatment. This prediction is not supported by clinical evidence and should be held.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Selenium is an essential micronutrient and a component of antioxidant selenoenzymes. No established therapeutic rationale links it to sclerosing cholangitis.

The literature only shows an association with the disease state. Patients with primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC) have altered hepatic selenium and copper levels, and their dietary intake is poor. One case report suggests that oxidative stress and low antioxidant status contribute to cholestatic liver disease. This is not evidence that giving selenium helps. The high TxGNN score should be treated as a hypothesis only.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- There are no clinical trials and no treatment studies in sclerosing cholangitis. The literature only describes altered selenium levels and diet in the disease, so the high model score is unsupported.
- Safety information from the SAHPRA PI has not been obtained, so safety screening cannot start.

**Other predicted indications with more evidence (for prioritisation):**
- **Congestive heart failure (L2):**
  - One completed Phase 3 trial, the LVAD perioperative high-dose selenium trial ([NCT02530788](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02530788), n=21). No outcome data were supplied.
  - Two large Phase 3 trials are not yet reporting: [NCT06694727](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06694727) (recruiting, n=4044) and [NCT07234422](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07234422) (not yet recruiting, n=1100). Both combine selenium with other supplements, so the selenium effect cannot be isolated.
  - Observational data suggest a U-shaped association, so both deficiency and excess may be harmful.
- **Rheumatoid arthritis (L3):** There is a 2025 meta-analysis of selenium supplementation and a 2023 Iranian clinical study on symptoms. Their designs are not confirmed. The one registered trial listed is irrelevant to RA, and the preclinical data are supportive only.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The SAHPRA package insert warnings and contraindications.
- Mechanism of action data, for example from DrugBank.
- The original approved indication and manufacturer details for the registered products.
- Any controlled human data for selenium in sclerosing cholangitis, if the indication is to be reconsidered. Otherwise, prioritise the heart failure question once the Phase 3 results are available.
