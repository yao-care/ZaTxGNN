## Amiodarone: Catecholaminergic polymorphic ventricular tachycardia

## One-Sentence Summary

Amiodarone is an oral antiarrhythmic that blocks potassium, sodium and calcium channels and also has beta-adrenergic blocking activity. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **catecholaminergic polymorphic ventricular tachycardia (CPVT)**. The data compiled for this report contain **no registered clinical trials** and **9 publications** on CPVT itself, and none of them tests amiodarone as a CPVT treatment.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the compiled data. Based on general pharmacology, amiodarone prolongs repolarisation by blocking potassium channels. It also blocks sodium and calcium channels and has beta-adrenergic antagonist activity. Together these actions suppress ventricular arrhythmias.

CPVT is a rare inherited arrhythmia caused by abnormal calcium release in heart cells (typically RYR2 or CASQ2 variants), triggered by exercise or emotional stress. The beta-blocking and partial calcium-channel effects of amiodarone could plausibly dampen this adrenergic-driven trigger. However, the link is indirect. Beta-blockers and flecainide are the established therapies, and the literature compiled here does not show CPVT-specific efficacy for amiodarone. The model score therefore reflects a pharmacological association, not demonstrated benefit.

## Safety Considerations

  - Thyroid: hypothyroidism, myxedema, congenital hypothyroidism, hyperthyroidism, Graves disease, thyrotoxicosis.
  - Lung: interstitial lung disease, eosinophilic pneumonia, pulmonary eosinophilia, hypersensitivity pneumonitis (extrinsic allergic alveolitis, farmer's lung).
  - Liver and gallbladder: liver disease, drug-induced liver injury, gallbladder disease.
  - Nerve and eye: peripheral neuropathy, peripheral nervous system disease, optic neuritis, optic nerve disease, visual pathway disease, corneal deposit.
  - Other: potassium deficiency.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- There are no registered trials, and none of the publications evaluates amiodarone in CPVT. Established alternatives (beta-blockers, flecainide) exist.
- The mechanistic link is plausible but indirect, and the safety profile requires caution, particularly in patients with conduction or QT disorders.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Studies or case series assessing amiodarone specifically in CPVT, compared with beta-blockers and flecainide
- Mechanism-of-action data
- The SAHPRA Professional Information (PI) warnings and contraindications, and the approved indication text for the two registered products
- A safety and monitoring plan covering QT, thyroid, liver and lung effects
