## Phenylephrine: Nasal cavity disease
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Phenylephrine is an alpha-1 adrenergic agonist originally used as a decongestant for conditions such as allergic rhinitis, the common cold and nasopharyngitis, and also in eye drops and haemorrhoid preparations.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **nasal cavity disease**, but there are currently **0 registered clinical trials** and only **5 publications**, mostly indirect or supportive, behind this prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, phenylephrine is a direct-acting alpha-1 adrenergic agonist. It constricts the blood vessels of the nasal mucosa, which reduces congestion, swelling and mucosal bleeding.

Nasal cavity disease is broadly an extension of the existing label. Phenylephrine is already associated with rhinitis, nasopharyngitis and the common cold, which are all conditions of the nasal cavity. The prediction is therefore mechanistically coherent, but it is closer to a broader description of the existing use than to a truly new indication.

The literature shows phenylephrine mainly as a component of combination nasal sprays (for example, Polydexa with phenylephrine) and as a topical vasoconstrictor in endoscopic sinus surgery. There is no direct evidence of phenylephrine as a single agent for nasal cavity disease. Topical vasoactive agents in the nose can be absorbed systemically and affect the cardiovascular system.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high (0.9997), but there are no registered clinical trials and the literature is indirect: combination products, a surgical adjunct study of another drug, and a case report. Nasal cavity disease also largely overlaps with phenylephrine's existing rhinitis and cold indications, so there is little that is new to evaluate. The same prediction approach also produced lower-ranked predictions with weaker support (acute laryngopharyngitis: no evidence; trigeminal autonomic cephalalgia: diagnostic pupillometry only, with no evidence of therapeutic benefit).

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SAHPRA Professional Information warnings and contraindications for the relevant phenylephrine products (a blocking data gap)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Controlled clinical studies of single-agent topical phenylephrine in specific nasal cavity conditions, with attention to blood pressure and systemic absorption
- Clarification of which registered products are relevant, and their approved indications
