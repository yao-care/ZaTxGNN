## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, mepyramine is a first-generation H1-receptor antagonist. Histamine released from activated mast cells drives the wheal-and-flare reaction in allergic urticaria, so blocking H1 receptors is biologically plausible.

The high TxGNN score is consistent with this class effect. However, this is largely a **class-effect indication, not a novel repurposing finding**, because H1 antihistamines are already the standard drug class for urticaria. Because no original indication or mechanism data were supplied, the link cannot be confirmed from this record alone.

TxGNN also ranked three other indications for mepyramine: nasal cavity disease (99.74%), acute laryngopharyngitis (99.72%) and cold urticaria (99.70%). They have weaker or no supporting evidence and are not assessed further here.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for safety information. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

The supplied literature describes serious effects of first-generation antihistamine overdose, including drowsiness, seizures, convulsions and coma. These are background observations, not label warnings.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is mechanistically plausible but is essentially a class effect. Support is limited to preclinical work and one veterinary study, with no human trials (L4). The safety review is also blocked, because the SAHPRA package insert warnings and contraindications are missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Download and review the SAHPRA package inserts (warnings, contraindications, active ingredient and strength in each product). This is a blocking gap.
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank.
- The registered indication text for each South African product.
- Human clinical evidence for mepyramine in urticaria, including a comparison with the second-generation antihistamines already used.
