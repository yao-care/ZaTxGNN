## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this report. Based on known drug class information, TAF is a prodrug of tenofovir, a nucleotide reverse transcriptase inhibitor. It delivers the active form of tenofovir efficiently into lymphoid cells after oral dosing.

SIV and the chimeric SHIV used in macaque studies are lentiviruses closely related to HIV-1 and depend on the same reverse transcriptase-driven replication cycle. It is therefore mechanistically plausible that TAF inhibits their replication. The literature compiled for this report shows macaque studies of pre-exposure prophylaxis, post-exposure prophylaxis and treatment, plus a humanised mouse model.

This is a research-model indication that supports the HIV use of the drug. It is not a new human therapeutic indication, and it does not by itself justify clinical development. Because the cited studies examine SHIV (a chimeric virus) or SIV model systems rather than a human disease, the evidence level is capped at L5.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only support for this prediction is preclinical macaque and mouse work in SIV/SHIV models, which has no registered human trials and no direct bearing on a new human indication. The model system also relies on the same reverse transcriptase mechanism that already underlies TAF's HIV use, so it adds little as a new clinical opportunity.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clarification of whether a research-model disease like SIV infection is relevant to the repurposing question at all, since it does not represent a distinct human indication
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- SAHPRA Professional Information (warnings, contraindications and approved indications) for the four registered products
- Review of the other predicted indications (feline acquired immunodeficiency syndrome, which is a veterinary condition outside human repurposing, and a genetic neurodevelopmental disorder with no plausible mechanistic link and likely a false positive), neither of which has any supporting trial or publication
