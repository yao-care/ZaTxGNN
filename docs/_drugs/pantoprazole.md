# Pantoprazole: From Peptic Ulcer Disease to Active Peptic Ulcer Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Pantoprazole is a proton pump inhibitor (PPI) originally used for peptic ulcer disease, gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GORD), oesophagitis and Zollinger-Ellison syndrome.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **active peptic ulcer disease**, with **15 clinical trials** (1 listed for this top-ranked indication and 15 for the related duodenal ulcer prediction) and **18 publications** currently supporting this direction.
This prediction is largely on-label or closely related to existing uses, so it is better seen as confirmation than as true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the literature retrieved, pantoprazole is a substituted benzimidazole PPI. It accumulates in the acidic compartment of the parietal cell, is acid-activated, and irreversibly inhibits the gastric H+/K+ ATPase (proton pump). This blocks the final step of acid secretion.

Peptic ulcers are acid-dependent, so suppressing acid allows healing and reduces recurrence. Because this is the same mechanism behind the drug's established peptic ulcer indication, the top prediction is on-label or closely related rather than a new therapeutic area.

The lower-ranked predictions are weaker. Peptic ulcer perforation and gastrojejunal ulcer are plausible only as acid-suppression adjuncts, and the retrieved literature is mostly about general ulcer disease or stress ulcer prophylaxis. For duodenal obstruction, the cause is typically mechanical and there is no clear mechanistic link. Its high model score is not supported by clinical evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (these are not label text): osteoporosis, postmenopausal osteoporosis, bone fracture and pernicious anaemia. These should be checked against the SAHPRA Professional Information (PI).
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified in the interaction screen. Examples of **Major** interactions include acalabrutinib and atazanavir. **Moderate** interactions include atorvastatin, bosutinib, fenofibrate, hydrochlorothiazide, alpelisib, amphetamine and apalutamide. **Minor** interactions include acetylsalicylic acid, axitinib, bortezomib, brivaracetam and cyanocobalamin. This list is a partial excerpt, and the full list should be reviewed before use.

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for warnings and contraindications. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple Phase 3 trials and several RCTs support pantoprazole in peptic and duodenal ulcer disease, and the product is already marketed in South Africa in oral and injectable forms. Because this is an on-label or closely related use, the main need is safe and appropriate use rather than new efficacy evidence. Other predictions (perforation, gastrojejunal ulcer, duodenogastric reflux) remain research questions, and duodenal obstruction should be put on hold.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SAHPRA PI warnings, contraindications and approved indication text (currently missing, and a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Confirmation of the mechanism of action from a primary source such as DrugBank
- Review of the full interaction list against local co-medications, especially atazanavir and acalabrutinib
- Attention to bone health and vitamin B12 status in long-term use, given the knowledge-graph signals
- Confirmation of the exact design and comparator of the key trials, since some titles and designs are truncated or unconfirmed
