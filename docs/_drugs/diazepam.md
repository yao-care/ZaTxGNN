# Diazepam: From Benzodiazepine Sedative-Anxiolytic to Insomnia

## One-Sentence Summary

Diazepam is a benzodiazepine sedative-anxiolytic that is registered as a tablet in South Africa. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **insomnia**, but **no trial has tested diazepam for insomnia**. The evidence is one small 1981 comparative trial, reviews, and mostly benzodiazepine-withdrawal studies, and the trials that mention benzodiazepines mostly concern stopping them.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Diazepam is a positive allosteric modulator of the GABA-A receptor. It enhances inhibitory signalling in the brain, which produces sedative and hypnotic effects. Benzodiazepines as a class are widely used for anxiety and insomnia (PMID 39581171), so a link between diazepam and insomnia is biologically plausible.

The supporting clinical data are thin:

- The only direct comparison is a 1981 double-blind study of 100 outpatients. In it, lormetazepam 1 mg outperformed diazepam 5 mg on time to fall asleep and sleep continuity (PMID 6113175).
- Most other material concerns the harms of long-term benzodiazepine hypnotic use: tolerance, dependence, altered sleep architecture, and cognitive impairment. This includes a mouse study linking chronic diazepam to impaired spine plasticity (PMID 35228700).
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data were not available in the Evidence Pack.

The model also produced a separate prediction for "sleep disorder, initiating and maintaining sleep". It is essentially the same clinical concept as insomnia and should be merged with this entry in review.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for safety information. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA. No warnings, contraindications, or drug interaction records were available in the Evidence Pack.

Signals from the supplied literature, not from the PI:

- Tolerance and dependence with long-term use
- Cognitive impairment, including in older adults
- Altered sleep architecture
- Abuse and misuse liability
- A reported association with breast cancer risk (observational)

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The GABA-A mechanism makes an insomnia effect plausible. However, no trial tests diazepam for insomnia, the only direct comparison is a 1981 study in which another benzodiazepine performed better, and the literature is dominated by dependence and withdrawal concerns. The SAHPRA package insert safety data are still missing, which blocks safety screening.

The other nine predicted indications are also on Hold, at evidence level L4–L5:

- **Cauda equina syndrome**, **ADHD** (both types), **specific developmental disorder** and **neurogenic bladder** have no supportive evidence. Neurogenic bladder is also an obsolete ontology term.
- **Antidepressant, hallucinogen and barbiturate abuse** show only indirect evidence about benzodiazepine dependence and withdrawal.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Download and review the SAHPRA package insert (warnings, contraindications, registered indication) for Benzopin 2 (L/2.6/90)
- DrugBank mechanism-of-action data to complete the mechanistic analysis
- Confirmation of whether insomnia is already on the registered label
- Head-to-head or placebo-controlled trial data for diazepam in insomnia, including comparison with current first-line options
- A risk plan for dependence, cognitive effects, and use in older adults
