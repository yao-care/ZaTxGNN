# Ampicillin: From Bacterial Infections to Laryngitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Ampicillin is a beta-lactam antibiotic used against susceptible bacterial infections. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **laryngitis**, but the supporting evidence is weak: **1 clinical trial** (a different drug, observational) and **20 publications** (mostly case reports and reviews, none testing ampicillin for laryngitis).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Ampicillin is a beta-lactam antibiotic, and its efficacy against susceptible bacterial infections is well established. Mechanistically, it could be relevant to laryngitis only when the cause is bacterial.

Most laryngitis is viral, and antibiotics do not help viral disease. Ampicillin is plausible only for bacterial laryngeal infections such as epiglottitis or laryngeal abscess. The high TxGNN score therefore does not translate into a clear indication for routine laryngitis.

The retrieved evidence does not test ampicillin for laryngitis. It consists of reports on related bacterial conditions, and the one registered trial studied a different drug.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The high TxGNN score is not backed by direct evidence. No trial tests ampicillin for laryngitis, and the only registered trial studied a different drug.
- Most laryngitis is viral, so antibiotic benefit is limited to rare bacterial laryngeal infections (for example epiglottitis and abscess), which are already managed as separate conditions.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The SAHPRA Professional Information (PI) for warnings, contraindications and approved indications
- Mechanism of action data
- A clear definition of the bacterial laryngeal subgroup where ampicillin might apply, plus local resistance data
- Comparative or randomised evidence in that subgroup, and drug interaction screening
