## One-Sentence Summary

Alprazolam is a benzodiazepine that enhances GABA-A receptor signalling, producing sedative and anxiolytic effects.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **insomnia**, with a prediction score of **0.9981**.
No clinical trials are registered, and the only study that directly addresses insomnia is a retrospective observational study (evidence level **L3**).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general benzodiazepine pharmacology, alprazolam enhances GABA-A receptor signalling, which produces sedative and anxiolytic effects. This gives a plausible mechanistic basis for sleep-promoting activity.

Insomnia is also commonly comorbid with anxiety and panic, so symptom overlap is likely.

However, the retrieved literature offers mostly indirect support. Most papers address panic disorder, benzodiazepine pharmacokinetics, utilisation trends, economic burden, zolpidem deprescribing and COVID-19 insomnia epidemiology. Long-term hypnotic use also carries risks of dependence, tolerance, falls and cognitive impairment, particularly in elderly patients.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only study that directly addresses insomnia is a retrospective observational study in patients with coronary heart disease (evidence level L3), and no randomised trial or registered trial exists. The dependence and fall risks of long-term hypnotic use also call for caution.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Randomised controlled trials of alprazolam specifically for insomnia
- Mechanism of action data
- Review of the SAHPRA Professional Information for warnings and contraindications
- A safety plan for dependence, tolerance and falls, especially in elderly patients
