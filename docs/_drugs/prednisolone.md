# Prednisolone: From Autoimmune Disease and Leukaemia to Alopecia Areata

## One-Sentence Summary

Prednisolone is a glucocorticoid originally used for autoimmune and inflammatory conditions (such as Crohn disease and adrenocortical insufficiency) and for acute lymphoblastic leukaemia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **alopecia areata**, with **no registered clinical trials** but **20 publications** currently supporting this direction, including a placebo-controlled RCT, a network meta-analysis and a systematic review.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, prednisolone is a glucocorticoid. Its efficacy in autoimmune and inflammatory diseases is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to alopecia areata.

Alopecia areata is a T-cell-mediated autoimmune attack on the hair follicle, with collapse of the follicle's immune privilege. Glucocorticoids suppress T-cell activation and inflammatory cytokine production, which matches this disease mechanism and the drug's existing autoimmune indications. One published study also suggests that oral pulse steroids lower serum and tissue TNF-α levels in alopecia areata.

Pulse and sequential regimens are already used in clinical practice. However, hair loss often relapses after withdrawal, and cumulative steroid toxicity limits long-term use.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Prednisolone is already marketed in South Africa. Alopecia areata has a strong immunological rationale, and the literature includes one placebo-controlled RCT, a Cochrane network meta-analysis and a systematic review supporting systemic corticosteroid use. No clinical trials are registered, and the evidence is limited by relapse after withdrawal and steroid toxicity with prolonged use. For these reasons the evidence level is L2.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SAHPRA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking data gap that must be resolved before safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- A review of the relevant RCT and comparison with newer therapies, such as JAK inhibitors, where these are available or accessible
- A pulse-dosing protocol with a relapse-management plan and monitoring for steroid adverse effects (blood pressure, glucose, growth in children, bone density, infection risk, adrenal suppression)
- Confirmation that the registered syrup formulation suits the intended dosing, since route and formulation compatibility has not been assessed
