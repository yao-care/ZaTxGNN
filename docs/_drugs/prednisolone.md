# Prednisolone: From Systemic Corticosteroid Therapy to Alopecia Areata

## One-Sentence Summary

Prednisolone is a systemic corticosteroid that is marketed in South Africa as a syrup. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **alopecia areata**. Support is mostly observational and review-level: **3 of the 16 retrieved trials** address alopecia, none has a prednisolone arm, and there are **20 publications**, including one placebo-controlled prednisolone pulse study.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, prednisolone is a systemic glucocorticoid with broad anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects. Its efficacy in other inflammatory and autoimmune conditions is well established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to alopecia areata.

Alopecia areata is a T-cell-mediated autoimmune attack on the hair follicle, so suppressing that inflammation is a plausible strategy. One mechanistic study (PMID 30294905) suggests oral pulse steroids may act partly by altering TNF-α levels in serum and tissue.

Relapse after withdrawal and cumulative steroid toxicity limit long-term use. Newer targeted agents, such as JAK inhibitors, are now the focus of most modern trials. The very high TxGNN score is therefore not matched by direct prednisolone trial evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic rationale is plausible and steroid pulse therapy is described in the alopecia areata literature. However, no listed trial tests prednisolone in alopecia areata. The direct evidence is observational or review-level, with one older placebo-controlled pulse study. Relapse and steroid toxicity limit long-term use. The SAHPRA package insert safety data are also missing, which blocks safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The SAHPRA package insert (warnings, contraindications, approved indications) for Adco prednisolone
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Appraisal of the 2005 placebo-controlled pulse prednisolone study (PMID 15692475), and any prednisolone-specific RCTs in alopecia areata
- A comparison with JAK inhibitors, given their access and eligibility limits in South Africa
- Confirmation of whether a syrup formulation suits the intended pulse regimens and age groups
- Reconciliation of the missing original-indication field with the registered label

For reference, the same evidence pack ranks idiopathic steroid-sensitive nephrotic syndrome at evidence level L1 (Proceed with Guardrails). That is guideline-standard prednisolone use rather than true repurposing, and it may merit its own review.
