# Tiotropium: From Inhaled Bronchodilator to Obstructive Lung Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Tiotropium is a long-acting inhaled antimuscarinic bronchodilator that is already registered and marketed in South Africa.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **obstructive lung disease**, and this prediction is supported by more than **40 registered clinical trials** and **20 publications**, including several large Phase 3 RCTs.
The evidence is strong, but most of it comes from COPD, so this is probably an on-label use rather than a true repurposing finding.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Tiotropium blocks muscarinic M1, M2 and M3 receptors. It dissociates slowly from M3 receptors, which sit on airway smooth muscle, so it works for a long time and can be given once daily. Blocking M3 relaxes airway smooth muscle and reduces cholinergic bronchoconstriction and mucus secretion. Structured mechanism-of-action data were not available in the Evidence Pack, so this description is drawn from the published drug reviews retrieved (for example PMID 12010082).

Obstructive lung disease is the broad disease group that includes COPD and, to a degree, asthma. Cholinergic tone drives airflow limitation in these conditions, so the mechanism fits directly. The trial evidence is consistent with this: tiotropium has been studied mainly in COPD, and also in severe persistent asthma, obliterative bronchiolitis and childhood wheezing.

The high TxGNN score is therefore biologically plausible. However, the SAHPRA-registered products (Spiriva Respimat and the HandiHaler-type capsules) sit in the respiratory bronchodilator category. This prediction should be verified against the approved Professional Information (PI) before it is counted as a repurposing candidate.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for safety information. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

No drug interaction records were found. The literature retrieved for the COPD prediction also raises the following topics. These are signals to review, not confirmed conclusions:
- **Cardiovascular risk**: a meta-analysis of RCTs (PMID 32274526) examined tiotropium and adverse cardiovascular events.
- **Dementia risk**: a 2025 cohort study (PMID 40388132) examined whether tiotropium initiation is linked to dementia risk.
- **Ocular effects**: studies (PMID 39198799, NCT06525051) examined effects on anterior chamber parameters and intraocular pressure.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple completed Phase 3 RCTs and Cochrane reviews support tiotropium in obstructive airway disease, and the drug is already marketed in South Africa. The main caution is that this is likely an established use rather than a new one, and safety data for the South African products are missing.

The other predictions in the Evidence Pack are weaker. COPD (rank 5) is also L1 and on-label. The severe early-onset COPD subtype (rank 4) has only indirect evidence and is a research question. Respiratory malformation and the other structural, radiologic or rare-immunodeficiency predictions have no supporting evidence and are best held.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The SAHPRA package insert warnings and contraindications, which are currently blocking the safety screen
- Confirmation of the approved indications on the PI, to establish whether this is on-label use
- Structured mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- A review of cardiovascular, anticholinergic and ocular safety signals before any expansion to new populations, such as asthma or paediatric wheezing
