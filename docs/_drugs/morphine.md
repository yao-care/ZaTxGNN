## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. In general, morphine is a mu-opioid receptor agonist that produces analgesia through central and peripheral pain modulation. This makes a role in myofascial pain mechanistically plausible.

The link between the original indication (diarrhoeal disease, where opioids slow gut motility) and the predicted one (a chronic musculoskeletal pain condition) is weak. They share only the opioid receptor pharmacology.

The retrieved literature does not test morphine as a treatment for myofascial pain syndrome. It mostly covers opioid use in related pain conditions (temporomandibular joint dysfunction, failed back surgery syndrome) and peri-procedural analgesia. Long-term opioid use for chronic musculoskeletal pain also carries risks of tolerance, hyperalgesia and dependence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high, but no clinical trials or direct clinical studies test morphine in myofascial pain syndrome. The risks of long-term opioid use (tolerance, hyperalgesia, dependence, respiratory depression) are significant for a chronic musculoskeletal condition.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SAHPRA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data (from DrugBank)
- Controlled clinical evidence of morphine versus established treatments in myofascial pain syndrome
- A risk-benefit assessment against non-opioid options, including opioid stewardship considerations
- Approved indication text and EML status for the South African registrations

### Note on Other Predicted Indications
