# Pravastatin: From Hyperlipidaemia and Atherosclerosis to HIV-Associated Dyslipidaemia

## One-Sentence Summary

Pravastatin is a statin (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor) originally used for hyperlipidaemia, atherosclerosis and stroke-related disorders.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful in **HIV infectious disease**. In practice this means managing HIV-associated dyslipidaemia and cardiovascular risk, not treating the virus itself.
This direction is supported by **12 clinical trials** and **20 publications**, although many of the trials are drug-interaction studies rather than efficacy studies.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Pravastatin belongs to the statin class, which inhibits HMG-CoA reductase and upregulates hepatic LDL receptors. Its efficacy in lipid disorders and atherosclerosis is well established.

HIV infection and antiretroviral therapy, particularly protease inhibitors, commonly cause dyslipidaemia and endothelial dysfunction. This raises the risk of premature coronary artery disease. Pravastatin's lipid-lowering and endothelial effects address these problems, so the predicted indication is a close extension of its existing use rather than an antiviral effect.

Pravastatin is not dependent on CYP3A4 metabolism. This gives it a more favourable interaction profile than many other statins when combined with boosted protease inhibitors. Some interactions still exist, for example with darunavir/ritonavir.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindication-related conditions (from the drug-disease knowledge graph, not label text):** these conditions warrant caution or review before use. The list includes:
- Liver disease, kidney disease and gallbladder disease
- Myopathy, muscular disease and rhabdomyolysis
- Thrombocytopenia and thrombocytopenic purpura
- Peptic ulcer disease, gout and acute urate nephropathy
- Hypercalcaemia, hyperparathyroidism, nephrolithiasis and nephrocalcinosis
- Haemophilia and anaemia
- Substance abuse or dependence

**Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were identified. Moderate-level interactions include:
- Clarithromycin and metronidazole
- Tinidazole
- Rosuvastatin and simvastatin (other statins)
- Eluxadoline, naltrexone and pectin

Many other interactions are of unknown severity. The interactions relevant to HIV care are those with protease inhibitors and other antiretrovirals, notably darunavir/ritonavir, as shown in the interaction studies above.

For warnings and label contraindications, please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI). Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several randomised trials, including a Phase 3 trial and double-blind pravastatin studies, plus systematic reviews, support pravastatin for dyslipidaemia in people with HIV. The evidence is mostly lipid-surrogate based, with several trials small or directed at drug interactions. The indication is therefore an extension of its current lipid use. The strict count of completed Phase 3 RCTs supports L2 rather than L1.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Retrieve the SAHPRA package inserts (warnings, contraindications, approved indications). This is a blocking gap for safety screening.
- Obtain mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank.
- Define interaction guidance for antiretroviral regimens used in South Africa, particularly protease inhibitor combinations.
- Look for outcome evidence beyond lipid surrogates, such as cardiovascular events.
- Frame the indication as HIV-associated dyslipidaemia and cardiovascular risk, not as antiviral treatment.
- Confirm Essential Medicines List status and local guideline positioning.
