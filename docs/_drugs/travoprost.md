# Travoprost: From Open-Angle Glaucoma to Visceral Calciphylaxis

## One-Sentence Summary

Travoprost is a prostaglandin (FP-receptor) analogue eye drop, originally used to lower eye pressure in open-angle glaucoma and ocular hypertension.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **visceral calciphylaxis**, but **no clinical trials and no publications** support this prediction. It is a computational signal only.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, travoprost is a topical prostaglandin FP-receptor agonist. Its efficacy in lowering intraocular pressure is established, and it has vasodilatory properties.

No plausible link to calcific uremic arteriolopathy (calciphylaxis) is documented. The very high score most likely reflects proximity to vascular-disease nodes in the knowledge graph rather than a validated mechanism. The original indication (an eye pressure disorder) and the predicted indication (a calcifying small-vessel disease, usually in kidney failure) share no known pathway.

Other top-ranked predictions are also computational only, with no trials or literature: venous, arterial and neurogenic thoracic outlet syndrome, angiodysplasia of stomach, blue toe syndrome, spontaneous coronary artery dissection and lymphangiectasis. The broader "vascular disease" prediction (score 99.9997%) and hemangioendothelioma are discussed below as the only ones with any retrieved material, and neither shows a therapeutic benefit.

## Safety Considerations

- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (from drug-disease links, not label text): uveitis, iris disease, ciliary body disease, panuveitis, iritis, cystoid macular oedema and macular retinal oedema.
- **Case report signal:** uveal effusion with exudative retinal detachment after topical travoprost in a patient with Sturge-Weber syndrome (a vascular malformation disorder). This argues for caution in patients with vascular malformations.
- **Common ocular effect:** conjunctival hyperaemia (reported in up to about 50% of patients in one review) and iris pigmentation changes.
- **Drug interactions:** 49 interactions were recorded, all with an "Unknown" severity level, for example calcitriol, pantoprazole, morphine, metformin, prednisone and aspirin. Their clinical significance is not established.

Please also refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction for visceral calciphylaxis rests on a model score alone, with no trials, no literature and no plausible mechanism. Retrieved studies concern glaucoma, and the one vascular-malformation report describes harm rather than benefit.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A mechanistic rationale linking FP-receptor agonism to calciphylaxis pathology (vascular calcification, thrombosis)
- Preclinical or case-level evidence of benefit
- Mechanism of action data, which is currently missing
- SAHPRA Professional Information warnings and contraindications (not yet reviewed)
- A route and formulation assessment, since the only registered forms are eye drops with negligible systemic exposure
