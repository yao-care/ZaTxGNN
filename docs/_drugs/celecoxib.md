# Celecoxib: From Arthritis Pain to Inflammatory Spondylopathy (Axial Spondyloarthritis)

## One-Sentence Summary

Celecoxib is a COX-2-selective NSAID. Published reviews describe its use for osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis and acute pain.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Inflammatory Spondylopathy** (axial spondyloarthritis and ankylosing spondylitis).
This is the best-supported of the top 10 predictions, with **17 registered trials** (2 completed Phase 3 randomised trials) and **20 publications**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the supplied record. Celecoxib is a selective COX-2 inhibitor. COX-2 inhibition reduces prostaglandin E2-driven inflammation and pain. This is the same mechanism through which celecoxib relieves arthritis symptoms.

Axial spondyloarthritis and ankylosing spondylitis are inflammatory rheumatic diseases with inflammatory back pain and stiffness. NSAIDs are a recognised first-line symptomatic therapy for them. The mechanism therefore fits.

There is also an emerging signal that celecoxib may slow radiographic bone progression (PMID 39757202). This is not yet established. Published reviews already list ankylosing spondylitis among celecoxib's approved uses in one or more countries. This may therefore not be true repurposing, so please verify the label status for South Africa.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for safety information. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

The supplied data has no warnings, contraindications or drug-interaction entries. The literature above points to cardiovascular risk, GI bleeding, and renal and hepatic function as the main areas to monitor with long-term use (PMIDs 40028763, 39315555).

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Two completed Phase 3 randomised trials (NCT00648141, NCT00762463) and several Phase 4 trials test celecoxib directly in ankylosing spondylitis. The mechanism is plausible. Celecoxib may already carry this indication in other jurisdictions, so the case for true repurposing is unclear.

The other top-10 predictions are weaker:
- Rank 8, rheumatoid factor-positive polyarticular JIA, is a research question only. Its single publication is a safety registry.
- Rank 3, rheumatoid vasculitis, has only a case report and no mechanistic support.
- The rest are model predictions with no supporting evidence or plausible mechanism. Keep them on Hold.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The SAHPRA package insert (warnings, contraindications, approved indications), to confirm whether ankylosing spondylitis is already labelled in South Africa
- Verification of the completed Phase 3 trials, given truncated titles and summaries
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- A safety monitoring plan for long-term use (cardiovascular, GI, renal and hepatic)
