## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacology, benzocaine is a topical ester local anaesthetic that blocks voltage-gated sodium channels, which reduces nerve signalling and produces a numbing effect.

A symptom-relieving anaesthetic effect on the ocular surface is conceivable. However, papillary conjunctivitis is typically allergic or related to contact lens wear, and an anaesthetic does not address those underlying causes. Topical benzocaine is not an accepted ocular treatment, and it carries risks of corneal toxicity and contact sensitisation. The high model score (0.9938) therefore has no clinical or literature backing at this time, and the mechanistic link should be regarded as weak and speculative.
