# Atorvastatin: From Hyperlipidaemia and Cerebrovascular Disease to Familial Hypercholesterolaemia

## One-Sentence Summary

Atorvastatin is a statin originally used to treat lipid disorders (such as hypertriglyceridaemia, hyperlipoproteinaemia and homozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia) and to reduce cardiovascular and cerebrovascular disease risk.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **familial hypercholesterolaemia**, with **14 clinical trials** and **18 publications** compiled for this direction.
Because homozygous familial hypercholesterolaemia is already among its listed original indications, this is effectively an on-label or closely related use rather than true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Atorvastatin inhibits HMG-CoA reductase, the key enzyme in cholesterol synthesis. This lowers hepatic cholesterol, upregulates LDL receptor expression and increases LDL-C clearance from the blood. Formal mechanism-of-action data were not available in the data compiled for this report, so this description relies on the mechanistic rationale and on the published literature.

Familial hypercholesterolaemia (FH) is caused mainly by impaired LDL receptor function. A drug that increases LDL receptor expression therefore has a clear rationale. Statins are the cornerstone of FH treatment, and atorvastatin is one of the most potent and widely used. Efficacy may be reduced in patients with null LDL receptor variants, who often need add-on therapy such as ezetimibe or PCSK9 inhibitors. This is reflected in several trials in which atorvastatin was background therapy.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph (not label text):** liver failure and liver disease, kidney disease, gallbladder disease, hypothyroidism (including myxoedema and congenital hypothyroidism), myopathy and other muscle diseases, rhabdomyolysis, familial periodic paralysis, hypotensive disorder, substance abuse/dependence, and injury. These should be confirmed against the approved Professional Information (PI).

**Drug interactions (100 recorded; main ones listed):**
- **Major:** Clarithromycin (CYP3A4 inhibition may raise atorvastatin exposure and the risk of myopathy).
- **Moderate:** aprepitant, cimetidine, clotrimazole, dexamethasone, eliglustat, eluxadoline, metronidazole, miconazole, naltrexone, pectin, rosuvastatin, simvastatin, and the proton pump inhibitors omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole and pantoprazole.
- **Minor:** aluminium hydroxide, magnesium hydroxide, liraglutide.

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for the full warnings and contraindications. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
- Multiple completed Phase 3 trials in familial hypercholesterolaemia (adult and paediatric) and supportive literature, including a South African open-label study, indicate that atorvastatin is effective in this setting. Many trials, however, test add-on drugs rather than atorvastatin alone, and this indication is largely on-label already.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SAHPRA Professional Information (warnings, contraindications and approved indications) for each registered product.
- Verification of the Precedex registration link and confirmation of EML status.
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank.
- A monitoring plan for liver function, muscle symptoms and interacting medicines, particularly in patients on CYP3A4 inhibitors or antiretrovirals.
- For patients with null LDL receptor variants, a plan for add-on therapy.
