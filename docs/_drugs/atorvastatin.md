## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Atorvastatin is a statin used to lower blood lipids, and it is also described in the literature as having anti-inflammatory (pleiotropic) effects, such as reduced monocyte and macrophage activation and lower immune activation markers.

People living with HIV have chronic residual inflammation and a higher risk of cardiovascular disease, and protease inhibitor–based antiretroviral regimens often raise cholesterol and triglycerides. This makes a statin a logical add-on in this population.

The strongest rationale is therefore managing HIV-associated dyslipidaemia, cardiovascular risk and immune activation. Evidence that atorvastatin affects viral replication, immune recovery or HIV-associated neurocognitive disorder (HAND) is weak and based on very small studies. The use is better framed as managing comorbidity than as treating HIV infection itself. Major drug interactions with protease inhibitors and boosters (CYP3A4 inhibition) need dose limits and monitoring.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions** (100 interactions recorded; main ones shown):
  - Major: clarithromycin
  - Moderate: aprepitant, omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, pantoprazole, cimetidine, clotrimazole, miconazole, dexamethasone, eliglustat, eluxadoline, metronidazole, naltrexone, pectin, rosuvastatin, simvastatin
  - Minor: aluminium hydroxide, magnesium hydroxide, liraglutide
- **HIV-specific interaction concern**: protease inhibitors and boosters inhibit CYP3A4, which raises statin exposure and the risk of myopathy and rhabdomyolysis. Dose limits and monitoring are required.
- **Conditions of concern**: the data flag liver failure and liver disease, kidney disease, myopathy and muscle disease, rhabdomyolysis, hypothyroidism, gallbladder disease and substance dependence. These come from relationship data, not from label text, so check them against the Professional Information.

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several small Phase 2–4 studies, including a completed Phase 3 trial in an African HAART cohort, support using atorvastatin for HIV-associated dyslipidaemia, cardiovascular risk and immune activation. The evidence for treating HIV infection itself is weak, and the trials are small, so any use should be framed as comorbidity management with strict interaction control.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results of the larger trial NCT03037372 (atorvastatin vs rosuvastatin in an African cohort, 320 participants), whose status is unknown
- SAHPRA Professional Information warnings and contraindications, and mechanism of action data
- A protocol for dose limits and monitoring with protease inhibitors and boosters (CYP3A4 interactions)
- Clear definition of the target outcome (lipids, cardiovascular events or immune activation) rather than viral control
