## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Atorvastatin inhibits HMG-CoA reductase, the key enzyme in hepatic cholesterol synthesis. This upregulates hepatic LDL receptors and lowers LDL cholesterol (LDL-C). FH is a genetic condition with markedly raised LDL-C and premature cardiovascular disease, and lowering LDL-C is the main treatment goal. The mechanistic link is therefore direct.

Because the original indication and mechanism fields are missing from the record, we cannot confirm whether FH is a label indication or a true repurposing candidate. It is very likely an existing use, so this prediction confirms known efficacy rather than a novel signal.

Response also differs by FH type. Homozygous FH usually needs add-on therapy, because LDL-receptor function is greatly reduced. Heterozygous FH generally responds better to statin therapy.

The score is not a discriminating signal. All six predictions for this drug score about 0.99, including ones with no supporting evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several completed Phase 3 trials, including randomised comparisons, involve atorvastatin in FH, and the mechanism is direct. However, atorvastatin is mostly the comparator or background therapy in these trials, and the finding is probably confirmation of known use rather than new repurposing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirm the approved indication from the SAHPRA Professional Information and check whether FH is already on the label.
- Obtain warnings, contraindications and interactions from the PI. The interaction query returned no results, which is a data gap and not evidence of no interactions.
- Distinguish heterozygous from homozygous FH in any protocol, since homozygous FH usually needs add-on therapy.
- Follow guideline dosing and monitoring, including paediatric dosing where relevant.
