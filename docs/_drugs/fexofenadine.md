## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Fexofenadine is known as a selective peripheral H1-receptor antagonist. A plausible link is attenuation of histamine-mediated inflammation of the ocular surface.

However, rosacea conjunctivitis is driven mainly by meibomian gland dysfunction, microbial or Demodex factors, and innate immune dysregulation. H1 blockade is therefore likely at best a minor adjunct, not a core treatment. The high TxGNN score (0.9985) probably reflects the model's proximity between this drug and related conjunctival conditions rather than independent evidence for this disease. The prediction should be treated as a hypothesis only.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests only on model output (Evidence Level L5). No trial or publication directly studies fexofenadine in rosacea conjunctivitis, and the disease mechanism makes a major benefit from H1 blockade unlikely.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A literature and trial registry search (including SANCTR and PACTR) specifically for antihistamines in rosacea-related ocular disease
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- The SAHPRA Professional Information (warnings and contraindications) for safety screening
- Assessment of route compatibility (oral tablets and suspension versus a possible ocular formulation)
- Specialist ophthalmology and dermatology input on the plausibility of use as an adjunct
