## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the source record. Fexofenadine is a peripherally selective H1-receptor antagonist. Mechanistically, it could at most relieve histamine-mediated itch and redness on the ocular surface.

This link is weak. Ocular rosacea is driven mainly by meibomian gland dysfunction, *Demodex* mites and innate immune inflammation. H1 blockade is unlikely to address these core processes. The high TxGNN score (99.85%) reflects patterns in the knowledge graph, not clinical or mechanistic proof. Oral fexofenadine also has limited penetration to the ocular surface.

The other top predictions are mostly eye-surface or eyelid conditions, such as blepharoconjunctivitis, viral conjunctivitis and punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis. Some have a possible symptom-level link through an allergic component. Others are likely knowledge-graph artefacts, such as infective urethral stricture and post-bacterial disorder. None has any supporting trial or publication.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on model score alone (evidence level L5), with no trials or literature. The plausible mechanism (H1 blockade) does not address the main causes of ocular rosacea, and an oral tablet is not an obvious fit for an ocular surface disease.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The SAHPRA package insert (warnings, contraindications and approved indications), which is currently a blocking gap for safety screening
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A literature and trial search for fexofenadine or other antihistamines in ocular rosacea and blepharoconjunctivitis
- A route assessment: whether an oral tablet is suitable, or a topical ocular formulation would be required
- If the symptom-level allergic component is to be pursued, a prioritised review of blepharoconjunctivitis and noninfectious eyelid dermatoses
