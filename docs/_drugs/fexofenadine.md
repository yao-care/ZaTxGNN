## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Fexofenadine is a selective peripheral H1-receptor antagonist. Its efficacy in allergic and atopic conjunctival conditions is established, so it may plausibly dampen histamine-mediated inflammation of the ocular surface.

The link to rosacea conjunctivitis is weak, however. This condition is driven mainly by meibomian gland dysfunction, microbial or *Demodex* factors, and innate immune dysregulation. Histamine blockade is therefore likely, at best, a minor adjunct. The high TxGNN score most probably reflects the condition's proximity to the approved conjunctivitis indications in the knowledge graph rather than independent evidence.

Other predictions in the top 10 are similarly weak. The most biologically plausible is **noninfectious dermatoses of the eyelid** (rank 10, score 95.84%), where allergic or urticarial eyelid disease involves histamine-mediated itching. Even there, topical corticosteroids, calcineurin inhibitors and allergen avoidance remain standard of care. Predictions such as viral conjunctivitis, parasitic eyelid infestation, ulcerative blepharitis and infective urethral stricture would offer at most symptomatic relief, or no credible benefit, because fexofenadine does not treat the underlying cause.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is supported only by the model score (L5), with no clinical trials or literature. The mechanistic link is weak because rosacea conjunctivitis is not primarily histamine-driven. Established treatments (lid hygiene, antimicrobials, topical therapy) address its actual causes.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Fexofenadine's mechanism of action data, and a clearer disease-specific rationale
- A systematic literature and trial search for fexofenadine or oral antihistamines in rosacea conjunctivitis and eyelid dermatoses
- The SAHPRA package insert (warnings, contraindications, approved indication) to complete safety screening
- A route-compatibility assessment, since the only local product is an oral tablet and ocular conditions are often treated topically
- If any indication is pursued, consider prioritising allergic or noninfectious eyelid dermatoses over rosacea conjunctivitis, starting with a small exploratory study
