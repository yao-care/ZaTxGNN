## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacology, hydrocortisone is a glucocorticoid that suppresses local immune activity and inflammatory cytokines.

Alopecia areata is an autoimmune, T-cell-mediated attack on hair follicles, so an immunosuppressive anti-inflammatory agent is mechanistically plausible. Corticosteroids are already used in this condition in clinical practice, mostly more potent agents than hydrocortisone.

An important caveat: the only randomised trial used hydrocortisone 1% cream as the weak comparator against clobetasol 0.05%. It shows hydrocortisone's relative standing against a potent steroid, not its own efficacy. Reports from 1955–1973 describe hair regrowth after intralesional hydrocortisone, but these are uncontrolled and outdated.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
A completed Phase 3 randomised trial directly studies alopecia areata, but hydrocortisone was only the comparator, and the other studies are historical or uncontrolled. The mechanism is plausible and the drug is widely available, but efficacy is unproven and more potent steroids are already used.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A controlled study with hydrocortisone as the investigational arm, compared against placebo or an established treatment
- Mechanism of action data and the SAHPRA Professional Information warnings and contraindications
- Clarification of the intended route (topical vs intralesional) and whether a locally registered product fits it
- A safety plan covering skin atrophy and systemic absorption, especially in children
