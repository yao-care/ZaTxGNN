## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this drug. Based on known biology, zinc is a cofactor for many enzymes, including copper/zinc superoxide dismutase, and may influence oxidative stress and inflammation, both of which are implicated in diabetic retinopathy. This link is plausible but unverified.

There are important caveats. Zinc chloride's usual exposure profile (systemic nutritional use, or topical use as an astringent) differs from ocular delivery. No evidence in the data compiled for this report supports retinal efficacy or safety. Any intravitreal or systemic use for this purpose would require extensive safety assessment.

The other two predictions are hypothesis-level only:
- **Sjogren syndrome:** zinc is involved in immune regulation and epithelial/mucosal integrity, which may relate to autoimmune exocrine gland dysfunction.
- **Dry eye syndrome:** zinc supports ocular surface epithelial repair and has anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties. This indication is closely related to Sjogren syndrome, which may explain the correlated scores. Zinc chloride is an astringent and potential irritant, so ocular tolerability would need to be established first.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The predictions rest solely on a computational score, with no clinical trials or literature, no mechanism of action data, and no SAHPRA label safety information. Zinc chloride's available formulations are not designed for ocular use, and its irritant properties raise tolerability concerns.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**

- Mechanism of action data (e.g. from DrugBank)
