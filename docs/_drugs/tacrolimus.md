# Tacrolimus: From Transplant Immunosuppression and Atopic Dermatitis to Seborrheic Dermatitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Tacrolimus is a calcineurin inhibitor. It is registered in South Africa as capsules and an infusion (systemic use) and as an ointment (topical use, Protopic). TxGNN predicts it may be effective for **seborrheic dermatitis**, and **2 completed clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently point in this direction. The published evidence includes a multicentre double-blind RCT of maintenance therapy on the face. This is a topical use only.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the supplied record. From the literature, tacrolimus is a calcineurin inhibitor. It reduces T-cell activation and the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines (PMID 15461548, PMID 11145792).

Seborrheic dermatitis is a chronic, relapsing inflammatory skin disease of sebaceous areas, mainly the face and scalp. Its inflammation is thought to be driven by an immune response to *Malassezia* yeasts, so an anti-inflammatory calcineurin inhibitor is plausible (PMID 19222250, PMID 15663338). Topical corticosteroids are the usual anti-inflammatory option. Their long-term facial use is limited by adverse effects such as skin atrophy, whereas topical calcineurin inhibitors do not cause dermal atrophy (PMID 19222250, PMID 11807435). This makes tacrolimus ointment a candidate for maintenance treatment, where relapse prevention is the aim.

The link is therefore anti-inflammatory and topical. It does not rely on tacrolimus's systemic immunosuppressive use.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
A completed Phase 3 trial and a completed Phase 4 trial target this exact indication and drug. A published multicentre double-blind RCT supports maintenance use on the face, and the mechanism is plausible. However, the trial results are not in the pack, no guideline endorsement is documented, and the SAHPRA safety data are missing, so a full "Go" is not justified.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SAHPRA PI warnings, contraindications and interaction data. This is a blocking gap for safety screening.
- Published results of NCT02004860 and NCT01591070, including relapse rates and safety.
- The registered indications of Protopic in South Africa, to confirm that seborrheic dermatitis is off-label.
- Guardrails: topical use only, on the face, within the label's age and duration limits. Consider the boxed warning on long-term topical use.
- Guideline or specialist dermatology review of maintenance regimens.
- A mechanism of action record from DrugBank.
