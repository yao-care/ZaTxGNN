# Tocopherol: From an Unspecified Registered Indication to Sclerosing Cholangitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Tocopherol (vitamin E) is registered in South Africa in two single-ingredient products, but the data compiled for this report do not state an approved indication.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **sclerosing cholangitis** (score 98.84%), but this prediction is supported by **no clinical trials** and only **1 publication**, which measured antioxidant levels rather than treatment effect.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Tocopherol is a fat-soluble vitamin with antioxidant properties. Based on general knowledge, it protects cell membranes from lipid peroxidation, and this may be applicable to conditions involving oxidative stress.

Chronic cholestatic liver disease causes malabsorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) and is associated with oxidative stress. The one available paper (Floreani et al., 2000) measured plasma antioxidant levels in these patients. It did not test whether tocopherol treats the disease. Any benefit would most likely be supportive replacement of a deficient vitamin rather than modification of the underlying disease.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top prediction (sclerosing cholangitis) rests on a single paper that measured antioxidant levels and did not test treatment. There are no registered trials. The drug is marketed in South Africa, but its approved indications and safety label data are not available in the data compiled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The SAHPRA Professional Information (indications, warnings, contraindications) for each tocopherol-containing registration, together with verification of the registration records
- Mechanism of action data (e.g., from DrugBank)
- For sclerosing cholangitis: clinical studies testing tocopherol on disease outcomes, not only vitamin levels
- For rheumatoid arthritis and peripheral vascular disease: well-designed randomized trials, as these have the most promising early evidence
- A safety review of high-dose vitamin E, particularly in cardiovascular patients
