## Triamcinolone: Alopecia mucinosa
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Triamcinolone is a corticosteroid originally used for autoimmune and inflammatory conditions such as Crohn disease, adrenocortical insufficiency and leukaemia-related indications.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Alopecia Mucinosa** (follicular mucinosis), but there are **no registered clinical trials** and only **2 case reports** supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, triamcinolone is a corticosteroid with anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive activity. Its efficacy in inflammatory and autoimmune conditions is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to alopecia mucinosa.

Follicular mucinosis involves perifollicular inflammatory infiltrate and mucin deposition. Intralesional corticosteroids are therefore a plausible approach for localised disease. However, support is limited to case-level reports. The only recent case (2014) describes a different agent, bexarotene gel, not triamcinolone. The very high TxGNN score is not backed by clinical data.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is biologically plausible, but evidence is limited to two case reports (one from 1964, and one involving a different drug) and no trials. The TxGNN score alone is not sufficient to support a clinical or regulatory move.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Case series or controlled studies of intralesional triamcinolone in follicular mucinosis, including exclusion of an underlying cutaneous T-cell lymphoma
- SAHPRA Professional Information (warnings, contraindications and approved indications) for each registered product
- Confirmation of whether an injectable (intralesional) triamcinolone product is registered in South Africa, since current registrations are topical or local forms
- Mechanism of action data for the formal mechanistic assessment
