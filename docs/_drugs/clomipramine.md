# Clomipramine: From Depression and Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder to Anxiety Disorder (Panic Disorder and Agoraphobia)

## One-Sentence Summary

Clomipramine is a tricyclic antidepressant known from the supplied literature for treating depression and obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD). The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **anxiety disorder**, with a score of 99.93%. This prediction is linked to **19 registered clinical trials** and **20 publications**. Most trials are in OCD, while the strongest support for anxiety comes from older controlled studies in panic disorder and agoraphobia.

L2 is assigned with a caveat. The only completed Phase 2/3 RCT in the pack (NCT03299166) tests troriluzole, not clomipramine. The clomipramine-specific support is published controlled studies in panic disorder and agoraphobia, with no registered Phase 3 trials.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the supplied record. The following comes from general pharmacology, not from the input. Clomipramine is a potent serotonin reuptake inhibitor. Its active metabolite, desmethylclomipramine, also inhibits norepinephrine reuptake. Serotonergic modulation is the accepted mechanism behind its effect in OCD and in panic and phobic anxiety.

The prediction is reasonable, but "anxiety disorder" is broader than the evidence. Of the 19 trials, most are in OCD, which DSM-5 no longer classes as an anxiety disorder. The evidence therefore supports the **panic disorder / agoraphobia** and OCD subsets, not anxiety disorders as a whole. Panic disorder with agoraphobia is the closest match to the prediction. A 2023 Cochrane network meta-analysis and several controlled comparisons against imipramine, diazepam, paroxetine and cognitive therapy cover it. Older reviews also describe clomipramine as one of the reference drugs for panic disorder.

## Safety Considerations

No warnings, contraindications or drug-interaction records were supplied. The interaction query returned no results. Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for safety information. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

The supplied literature mentions these signals, which are not a substitute for the PI:
- **Seizures:** a 1991 review cites a 0.4% incidence of seizures.
- **Anticholinergic effects:** these are described as common with clomipramine.
- **Case reports:** clomipramine-induced pancytopenia (PMID 3572384) and clomipramine-induced tics in a patient with OCD (PMID 9316681).

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Published controlled studies support clomipramine in panic disorder and agoraphobia, and in OCD. The broad label "anxiety disorder" is not supported, because the registered trials are mainly OCD and none is a clomipramine-specific Phase 3 trial in anxiety. The safety data for South Africa is also missing, which blocks progression to safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SAHPRA package insert warnings and contraindications for Clomidep (Reg. No. 35/1.2/0253), and its currently approved indication.
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank.
- A narrower target indication (panic disorder with or without agoraphobia) instead of "anxiety disorder".
- A review of the full text of the panic disorder RCTs and the 2023 Cochrane network meta-analysis for clomipramine-specific efficacy and tolerability.
- A safety monitoring plan covering cardiac conduction, seizure risk, anticholinergic burden, and suicidality in younger patients.

Other predicted indications in the pack are stronger or weaker than this one:
- **Agoraphobia, major depressive disorder and endogenous depression** reach L2 with the same Proceed with Guardrails recommendation. Depression is closer to confirming an existing use than to repurposing.
- **ADHD** is a research question only.
- **Benign paroxysmal torticollis of infancy and the personality disorders** are on Hold.
