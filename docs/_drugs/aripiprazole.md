# Aripiprazole: From Bipolar Disorder and Schizophrenia to Major Affective Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Aripiprazole is an atypical antipsychotic, originally used for bipolar disorder (including mania), schizophrenia and autism-related conditions.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **major affective disorder** (major depressive disorder, MDD), with **35 clinical trials** and **19 publications** compiled for this direction, including several large completed Phase 3 trials.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. From general pharmacological knowledge, aripiprazole is a dopamine D2/D3 partial agonist and a serotonin 5-HT1A partial agonist, with 5-HT2A antagonism. This profile can adjust dopamine and serotonin signalling when added to an antidepressant.

Its established indications (bipolar disorder, including mania) are themselves affective disorders, so the clinical and biological overlap with depression is strong. The prediction is supported by large, completed adjunctive-therapy trials. Reviews note that atypical antipsychotics, including aripiprazole, are among the most widely studied augmentation agents for treatment-resistant depression.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindication-related conditions (from the knowledge graph, not label text).** The drug-disease contraindication links in the knowledge graph include:
- **Metabolic:** diabetes mellitus, hyperglycaemia, diabetic ketoacidosis, hyperlipidaemia, obesity
- **Haematological:** neutropenia, agranulocytosis, leukopenia
- **Cardiovascular:** orthostatic hypotension, myocardial infarction, coronary artery disease, heart conduction disease, sudden cardiac arrest
- **Neurological:** epilepsy, cerebrovascular disorder, neuroleptic malignant syndrome

**Drug interactions.** The query returned 100 interactions, and the first 20 are in the data compiled for this report.
- **Major:** bupropion. This is relevant because bupropion is a common antidepressant partner, and the VAST-D trial compared the two.
- **Moderate:** antidiabetic agents (metformin, pioglitazone, acarbose, alogliptin, canagliflozin, albiglutide, acetohexamide, chlorpropamide), anticholinergics (atropine, hyoscyamine, clidinium), famotidine, cimetidine, epinephrine, loperamide, aprepitant, cisapride, clarithromycin and clotrimazole.

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for official warnings and contraindications. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several large, completed Phase 3 placebo-controlled trials and recent network meta-analyses support adjunctive aripiprazole in major depressive disorder, so the evidence is L1. Guardrails are needed because the SAHPRA label data, the registered oral formulation and the formal local indication are not confirmed, and the knowledge graph flags metabolic, haematological and cardiovascular cautions.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The SAHPRA package insert (warnings, contraindications and approved indication), which is currently missing and blocks formal safety screening
- Confirmation of whether an oral aripiprazole product is registered in South Africa, since the only registration found is an injection
- Verified mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- A monitoring plan covering metabolic parameters (glucose, lipids, weight), blood counts and cardiovascular status
- Interaction review for patients on bupropion or antidiabetic therapy
