# Ropinirole: From Parkinson Disease and Restless Legs Syndrome to Attention Deficit-Hyperactivity Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Ropinirole is a dopamine agonist originally used to treat Parkinson disease and restless legs syndrome (RLS).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)**,
but there are **no registered clinical trials** in the data compiled for this report. Support comes from **8 publications**, mostly reviews, a single paediatric case report, preclinical work, and one recent 2026 study whose design could not be verified.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on the information compiled, ropinirole is a dopamine D2/D3 agonist (with some D4 activity). Its efficacy in Parkinson disease and RLS is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to ADHD, which is associated with dopaminergic dysregulation.

RLS and ADHD frequently occur together and may share dopaminergic and iron-related pathways. This is the most plausible link, particularly for children or adults with both conditions. Preclinical models (A11-lesion/iron-deprivation) and work on D4/α2A-adrenoceptor heteromers support the underlying biology.

The clinical evidence is thin. It consists of one paediatric case report (a 6-year-old with ADHD and RLS whose attention and sleep symptoms improved on ropinirole) and reviews of the RLS-ADHD association. A 2026 study of ropinirole added to methylphenidate versus placebo in 66 children and adolescents appears to be a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. Its results were not available to us and its design could not be fully verified, so it is not counted as Phase 2/3 evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications and disease associations (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: multiple system atrophy, postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome, orthostatic hypotension, hypotensive disorders, drug-induced dyskinesia, liver failure, and psychotic disorders (psychotic disorder, schizophreniform disorder, schizoaffective disorder, mental disorder).
- **Drug interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Those rated Moderate include morphine (including liposomal), cimetidine, dronabinol, nabilone, opium, obeticholic acid, and metoclopramide. Other flagged drugs are of unknown severity, including pantoprazole, glimepiride, doxycycline, and sucralfate.
- **Literature-derived concerns**: dopamine agonists, including ropinirole, are associated with an increased risk of impulse-control problems such as gambling disorder, and can worsen psychosis. Both matter when treating a paediatric or psychiatric population.

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for formal warnings and contraindications. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (research question)**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high, and there is a plausible dopaminergic link, especially in children with comorbid RLS. However, the evidence is limited to reviews, a single case report, preclinical data, and one unverified 2026 study, with no registered trials. The drug is already marketed in South Africa, but this is not a registered indication, and the label safety information is not yet reviewed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Full-text review of the 2026 add-on trial (design, outcomes, adverse events) and confirmation of its registration
- Replication in adequately powered, registered randomised trials, ideally including children with comorbid RLS
- Retrieval and review of the SAHPRA Professional Information (warnings, contraindications, indications) and detailed mechanism of action data
- A safety plan covering impulse-control disorders, psychosis risk, orthostatic hypotension, and interactions with co-medications, including methylphenidate
