## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, budesonide is a glucocorticoid with broad anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive activity. Its efficacy in asthma, allergic rhinitis and inflammatory bowel disease is established.

Atopic eczema is driven by Th2-type inflammation and eosinophil involvement. Glucocorticoids suppress Th2 cytokines and eosinophil recruitment, so the mechanism is biologically plausible. Topical corticosteroids are already a standard treatment class for atopic eczema.

The high prediction score is probably driven by this class effect, not by budesonide-specific data. The retrieved literature is largely tangential. It covers eosinophilic oesophagitis, contact allergy to corticosteroids and adrenal suppression. Only one preclinical paper (budesonide nanoparticle hydrogels for atopic dermatitis) is directly relevant.

  - **Moderate:** zidovudine, isotretinoin, acebutolol, ketorolac, nifedipine, ibuprofen, ethinylestradiol, aldesleukin, alemtuzumab, fluvoxamine.
  - **Minor:** formoterol, salbutamol.

  - Contact allergy to corticosteroids is well documented, and budesonide is used as its marker allergen.
  - Systemic absorption of topical corticosteroids can suppress growth in children and the HPA axis.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is computational only (L5). There are no clinical trials for atopic eczema, and the only directly relevant paper is a preclinical formulation study. Established topical corticosteroids and other eczema therapies already exist, and no dermal budesonide product is registered in South Africa.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The SAHPRA Professional Information, to confirm warnings, contraindications and approved indications.
- Detailed mechanism of action data.
- Controlled clinical evidence of budesonide in atopic eczema, since none currently exists.
- A suitable dermal formulation and an assessment of its systemic absorption, particularly in children (growth and HPA axis).
- A hypersensitivity risk assessment, given the documented contact allergy to budesonide.

**Note on other predictions:** The second-ranked prediction, **bronchitis** (score 99.81%), has stronger support. It has Phase 2 trials of inhaled budesonide/formoterol in bronchiolitis obliterans (NCT00624754 and NCT01560689, each n=32) and supportive publications, giving an evidence level of L2. It is better suited to a focused follow-up review, since the registered inhaled products already match its route of administration.
