## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Candesartan blocks the angiotensin II type 1 receptor. Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report, but the pharmacology is well established. Angiotensin II signalling is thought to influence migraine through cerebrovascular tone, neurogenic inflammation, oxidative stress and sympathetic activity.

Blood pressure–lowering agents are already used in migraine prevention, and candesartan appears in guideline-style reviews as a preventive option. The placebo-controlled randomised trials listed below directly test candesartan in migraine prevention, which supports the model's prediction.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction search completed, with no specific interacting drugs identified. The only record is pharmacological: candesartan acts on the AT1 receptor (gene AGTR1).
- **Suggested guardrails** (from the repurposing assessment): monitor blood pressure, renal function and potassium, and avoid use in pregnancy because angiotensin receptor blockers are fetotoxic.

Please also refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

Two randomised placebo-controlled trials (NCT00884663 and NCT04574713, the latter published in The Lancet Neurology in 2025) and an observational study support candesartan for migraine prevention. There are no multiple Phase 3 trials, so the evidence level is L2.
