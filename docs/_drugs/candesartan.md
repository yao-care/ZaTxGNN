# Candesartan: From Hypertension to Migraine Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Candesartan is an angiotensin II type 1 receptor blocker (ARB), a class normally used for hypertension. The SAHPRA indication text was not supplied in the data.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine disorder** (preventive treatment).
Support comes from **3 directly relevant clinical trials** (including a completed Phase 2 RCT with 450 patients) and **20 publications**, including a 2025 *Lancet Neurology* RCT.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available from DrugBank. Based on known pharmacology, candesartan is an AT1 receptor blocker. It is proven in cardiovascular disease, and mechanistically it may be applicable to migraine prevention.

Proposed mechanisms include modulation of cerebrovascular tone, neurovascular inflammation and trigeminovascular signalling through the renin-angiotensin system. These are hypotheses, not established facts.

Clinical support is stronger than for most TxGNN predictions. A completed, triple-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 2 RCT (n=450) and a smaller Phase 2/3 comparison against propranolol (n=72) both address migraine prevention directly. No adequately powered Phase 3 trial is present, so the evidence level is capped at L2.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction query returned no results, which means "not found", not "no interactions".
- **Guardrails from the evidence pack**:
  - **Pregnancy:** ARBs are fetotoxic (case reports and reviews describe oligohydramnios, pulmonary hypoplasia and renal damage; e.g. [PMID 15669052](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15669052/)). Women of childbearing potential need contraception counselling.
  - **Blood pressure:** Watch for hypotension in normotensive migraine patients.
  - **Monitoring:** Renal function and potassium.

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
A completed placebo-controlled Phase 2 RCT (n=450), a Phase 2/3 active-comparator trial and supportive real-world cohorts point to a real signal in migraine prevention. The drug is already marketed in South Africa. However, there is no adequately powered Phase 3 trial, and the SAHPRA safety information is missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Extract warnings and contraindications from the SAHPRA package insert (a blocking gap for safety screening).
- Confirm the approved indication text for both registered products.
- Review the full results of NCT04574713 / PMID 40975098 (effect size and adverse events) and confirm the 8 mg and 16 mg doses against locally available strengths.
- Define a monitoring plan covering blood pressure, renal function, potassium and pregnancy prevention.
- Obtain mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank.

**Other predictions:** The other nine predicted indications (migraine with brainstem aura, migraine susceptibility, pulmonary hypertension, Prinzmetal angina, kyphoscoliotic heart disease, atrophoderma vermiculata, ulerythema ophryogenesis, benign prostatic hyperplasia and alopecia) are not supported by candesartan-specific clinical evidence. They should stay on Hold or be treated as research questions. The migraine susceptibility entry should be merged with the main migraine entry.
