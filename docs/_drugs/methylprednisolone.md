# Methylprednisolone: From Autoimmune Disease to Alopecia Areata

## One-Sentence Summary

Methylprednisolone is a systemic glucocorticoid originally used for autoimmune and inflammatory conditions such as Crohn disease, adrenocortical insufficiency and acute lymphoblastic leukaemia.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this report. Based on known information, methylprednisolone is a glucocorticoid with broad anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects, and its efficacy in autoimmune and inflammatory disease is well established.

Alopecia areata is a T-cell-mediated autoimmune disease in which the immune privilege of the hair follicle collapses. High-dose pulse glucocorticoid therapy is thought to suppress the autoreactive T-cell infiltrate around the follicle. Because the drug's original indications already include autoimmune disease, the biological rationale is strong.

The main guardrail concerns are relapse after stopping treatment and steroid adverse effects, particularly with repeated pulses.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible and supported by one completed Phase 4 trial plus multiple open-label and retrospective studies and reviews. However, no Phase 3 RCT is available, relapse after discontinuation is common, and steroid toxicity is a concern with repeated pulses.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SAHPRA Professional Information warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Confirmation of the NCT01167946 design and results, ideally with controlled comparative data
- A relapse-management and steroid-toxicity monitoring plan, including glucose and blood pressure monitoring and review of interacting drugs
- Confirmation of the approved indications and route suitability of the registered injectable products for this use
