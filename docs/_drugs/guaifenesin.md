## Guaifenesin: From Rhinitis and Common Cold to Nasal Cavity Disease
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Guaifenesin is an expectorant, originally used for symptomatic relief of upper respiratory conditions such as rhinitis, pharyngitis and the common cold.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **nasal cavity disease**,
but at present there are **0 clinical trials** and only **1 publication** (a 2002 review) supporting this direction, so the prediction rests mainly on the model.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, guaifenesin is an expectorant that thins and loosens airway mucus. Its efficacy in rhinitis and common cold-type conditions is the basis of its existing use, and mechanistically it may be applicable to nasal cavity disease through improved mucus clearance.

Nasal cavity disease is closely related to the original indications (rhinitis, nasopharyngitis). The high TxGNN score largely reflects this overlap with existing indications, so it should not be read as independent evidence of a new benefit. The only retrieved publication is a 2002 review on respiratory allergies in voice users, which mentions that decongestants containing guaifenesin may be useful. This is indirect, treatment-strategy-level support only.

**Other predictions reviewed:** Of the ten predicted indications, **tracheal disease** (score 98.83%, L4) is the most biologically plausible. An in vitro study (PMID 23113953) showed guaifenesin's effects on MUC5AC and mucociliary transport in human airway cells. It is flagged as a research question, but no clinical trials are registered. **Acute laryngopharyngitis** (99.98%) is closely related to existing indications but has no retrieved evidence. The remaining predictions (for example faucial diphtheria, cervical disc degenerative disorder, anorectal stricture and anal polyp) have no plausible mechanism and appear to be knowledge-graph artefacts.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction for nasal cavity disease overlaps heavily with guaifenesin's existing rhinitis and common cold indications, and the only supporting evidence is one indirect 2002 review with no registered clinical trials. The evidence does not yet justify moving forward with a new indication.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clinical studies (ideally randomised controlled trials) of guaifenesin in nasal cavity disease, or confirmation that the indication adds something beyond current rhinitis use
- Mechanism of action data to support the mucus-clearance rationale
- SAHPRA package insert warnings and contraindications, which have not yet been obtained and are needed for safety screening
- Approved indication text for the registered products, to clarify the current labelled use
- Consideration of tracheal disease as a separate research question, starting with clinical validation of the in vitro mucociliary findings
