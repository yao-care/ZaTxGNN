# Guaifenesin: From Expectorant Use to Nasal Cavity Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Guaifenesin is a marketed expectorant used to loosen airway mucus.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **nasal cavity disease**, with a direct signal in pediatric chronic rhinitis.
Support is limited: **1 completed Phase 2 pilot trial** and **2 review-type publications**, with no results supplied.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on general pharmacology, guaifenesin is an expectorant thought to thin and mobilise airway mucus. Its established use is in respiratory mucus conditions, and mechanistically it may be applicable to mucus-related upper-airway disease such as chronic rhinitis.

The link between the original and predicted indications is anatomical and functional. Both involve mucus and secretion clearance along the respiratory tract. Supporting evidence for the biology comes from a laboratory study of guaifenesin on MUC5AC (a mucus protein) and mucociliary transport in human tracheal-bronchial cells (PMID 23113953). This is indirect, in vitro, and not nasal, so it cannot show clinical benefit in nasal disease.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only direct evidence is one small, completed Phase 2 pilot in children with chronic rhinitis. Its design and results are not confirmed, so the evidence stays at L3 rather than L2. The high TxGNN score is a computational prediction and not clinical proof. Safety information has not yet been reviewed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results and design details (randomisation, control arm, outcomes) for NCT01364467, or a publication from it. A positive randomised result would support upgrading to L2.
- SAHPRA package insert warnings and contraindications, which must be reviewed before any safety screening.
- Curated mechanism of action data from DrugBank.
- Approved indication text for the registered South African products.

The other nine predictions are weaker. Tracheal disease reaches L4 on in vitro data only. The remaining eight are L5, with no trial or literature support, and are also on Hold.
