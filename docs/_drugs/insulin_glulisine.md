# Insulin Glulisine: From Diabetes Mellitus Glycaemic Control to Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus

## One-Sentence Summary

Insulin glulisine (Apidra) is a rapid-acting human insulin analogue used at mealtimes to improve glycaemic control in diabetes mellitus.
The TxGNN model predicts it for **type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM)**, and this is effectively its established labelled use rather than a true repurposing.
Evidence retrieved includes **50 registered clinical trials** (many in type 2 diabetes or otherwise off-target) and **19 publications**, including several randomised T1DM trials.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Based on known pharmacology, insulin glulisine is a rapid-acting human insulin analogue that binds the insulin receptor and lowers blood glucose. In T1DM it replaces the missing mealtime (prandial) insulin.

The SAHPRA record supplied lists no approved indication text, and no original indication was captured in the pack. The published literature describes glulisine as approved for improving glycaemic control in adults, adolescents and children with diabetes mellitus. The T1DM prediction therefore reflects the drug's existing use. The high TxGNN score is consistent with this, but it should not be read as a new-indication signal.

The other nine predicted indications (ranks 2 to 10) have no trials or literature in the pack. Most look like knowledge-graph artefacts:
- **Comorbidity links:** stiff person syndrome, autoimmune oophoritis.
- **Insulin as the cause:** injection-site lipodystrophy and lipoatrophy.
- **Most biologically coherent:** pancreatic agenesis, where insulin replacement is standard care for neonatal insulin-deficient diabetes. A targeted literature search would be a reasonable next step.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
At least two completed Phase 3 randomised trials in T1DM support glulisine (NCT00290979, NCT00271284), backed by randomised literature (PMID 16308840) and paediatric data. This meets L1, but it confirms an established use rather than a new indication. Proceeding is conditional on safety review, because the SAHPRA PI has not yet been obtained.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Download and review the SAHPRA package insert for warnings, contraindications and the approved indication text (a blocking gap for safety screening).
- Verify the T1DM vs T2DM population of each cited trial against its registry record.
- Retrieve DrugBank mechanism-of-action data.
- If the aim is genuine repurposing, run a targeted search on neonatal diabetes and pancreatic agenesis. The other low-ranked predictions should stay on hold.
