# Insulin Glulisine: From Diabetes Mellitus to Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus

## One-Sentence Summary

Insulin glulisine is a rapid-acting human insulin analogue, originally used to treat diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis.
The TxGNN model predicts it is effective for **type 1 diabetes mellitus**, with **29 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.
Type 1 diabetes is part of the drug's core use, so this is closer to confirmation of an existing use than true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, insulin glulisine is a rapid-acting insulin analogue that binds the insulin receptor and lowers blood glucose. Its use in diabetes mellitus is established, and mechanistically it applies directly to type 1 diabetes, where endogenous insulin is lacking.

Type 1 diabetes falls within the drug's original indication of diabetes mellitus, so the prediction is on-label use rather than a new therapeutic direction. Many completed trials in type 1 diabetes, including paediatric and insulin pump studies, are consistent with this.

Other lower-ranked predictions (for example thiamine-responsive dysfunction syndrome, stiff person syndrome, autoimmune oophoritis and pancreatic agenesis) have no supporting trials or literature. Most appear to reflect diabetes comorbidity rather than a treatment effect. Localized lipodystrophy and lipoatrophy are known injection-site adverse effects of insulin, so they are safety concerns, not opportunities. Pancreatic agenesis is the only one with a plausible rationale: insulin replacement is standard care, but there is no glulisine-specific evidence.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug Interactions:** The interaction screen returned 100 interacting drugs, mostly of Moderate severity. Examples:
- **Other glucose-lowering drugs:** metformin, pioglitazone, alogliptin, albiglutide (may increase hypoglycaemia risk)
- **Sympathomimetics and bronchodilators:** epinephrine, salbutamol, formoterol, phenylephrine, pseudoephedrine
- **Beta-blocker:** acebutolol
- **Corticosteroids and hormones:** hydrocortisone, estradiol, ethinylestradiol
- **Others:** acetazolamide, hydrochlorothiazide, doxycycline, alpelisib, ethanol
- Topical corticosteroids (for example hydrocortisone and alclometasone topical) are Minor.

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for warnings and contraindications. Injection-site lipodystrophy and lipoatrophy are recognized with injected insulins, so injection sites should be rotated. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.
