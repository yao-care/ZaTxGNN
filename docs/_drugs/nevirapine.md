# Nevirapine: From HIV Infection to Simian Immunodeficiency Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Nevirapine is an HIV-1 non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NNRTI), originally used to treat HIV infection and AIDS.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV) infection**, but there are **0 clinical trials** and only **indirect, preclinical literature** behind this prediction. SIV is a nonhuman primate disease, and the evidence suggests that native SIV is naturally resistant to nevirapine.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Nevirapine is known to be an HIV-1 NNRTI: it binds the HIV-1 reverse transcriptase (RT) enzyme and blocks viral DNA synthesis. SIV is a closely related lentivirus, which probably explains the high model score through disease similarity to HIV.

However, the mechanistic link is weak. SIV reverse transcriptase is naturally resistant to most HIV-1-specific NNRTIs, including nevirapine. Published work shows that HIV-2 and SIV are generally insensitive to these agents. Chimeric SIV/SHIV constructs carrying the HIV-1 RT gene are sensitive, which makes them useful as animal models for studying NNRTI activity and resistance. This does not show that nevirapine treats native SIV infection.

The high score therefore likely reflects similarity to HIV rather than true efficacy. SIV also has no human clinical relevance as an indication, so this prediction is of limited practical value for South African patients.

## Safety Considerations

Nevirapine's label warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI). Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

- **Conditions linked to contraindication or toxicity in the knowledge graph** (not label text): liver failure, liver disease, drug-induced liver injury, gallbladder disease, chronic hepatitis B and C virus infection, rash (exanthem), Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis (Lyell syndrome), rhabdomyolysis and nodular fasciitis. Hepatotoxicity and severe skin reactions are the main concerns.
- **Drug interactions**: 100 interactions are recorded. The 20 reviewed were all of Moderate severity and include rabeprazole, bupropion, aprepitant, clarithromycin, the corticosteroids (dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, prednisolone, triamcinolone), saxagliptin, linagliptin, ondansetron, granisetron, naltrexone, naloxegol, naldemedine, cisapride, eliglustat, nitisinone and orlistat. Many of these reflect nevirapine's induction of liver enzymes (CYP3A4/2B6), which can lower the levels of co-administered drugs.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The evidence is preclinical or indirect only (L4), with no clinical trials. SIV is naturally resistant to nevirapine, and SIV is a nonhuman primate disease with no human clinical relevance. The high TxGNN score most likely reflects similarity to HIV rather than true efficacy.

**Other predictions reviewed (also Hold):**
- **Feline acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (score 99.85%, L4):** One biochemical/structural study of NNRTIs against feline immunodeficiency virus (PMID 38031646) exists. Its title alone does not show that nevirapine inhibits the virus, and differences in the NNRTI binding pocket suggest limited activity. This is a veterinary indication.
- **Neurodevelopmental disorder with ataxic gait, absent speech, and decreased cortical white matter (score 99.82%, L5):** There are no trials or literature and no identifiable mechanistic link. Any hypothesis, such as involvement of retroelement reverse transcriptase, would be speculative. Nevirapine's hepatotoxicity, skin toxicity and many interactions weigh against repurposing without supporting evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- SAHPRA Professional Information (warnings and contraindications), since the lack of safety data blocks safety screening
- Direct evidence of nevirapine activity against the target virus or condition, which is unlikely for SIV given its natural resistance
- Prioritising other candidate indications that are relevant to human disease
