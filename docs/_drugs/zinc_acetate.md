# Zinc Acetate: From Wilson Disease and Skin Conditions to Severe Nonproliferative Diabetic Retinopathy

## One-Sentence Summary

Zinc acetate is a zinc salt whose recorded uses include Wilson disease, inflammatory skin conditions, burns and other disorders of metal metabolism.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **severe nonproliferative diabetic retinopathy**,

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Zinc acetate is a zinc salt, and zinc is an essential trace element. Its recorded uses in Wilson disease and metal-metabolism disorders are well recognised.

One plausible link is that zinc is a cofactor of Cu/Zn superoxide dismutase, an antioxidant enzyme. Oxidative stress is thought to contribute to microvascular injury in the diabetic retina, so zinc could be relevant. This reasoning is speculative. None of the data compiled for this report tests it. The high score reflects proximity in a knowledge graph, not demonstrated efficacy.

The model also ranked diabetic retinopathy (score 99.92%) as a closely related prediction. It has the same absence of evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (from knowledge graph, not label text)**: The knowledge graph links zinc acetate to contraindication with porphyria conditions (inherited porphyria, porphyrin metabolism disease, porphyria).
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified in total, and the 20 listed in the compiled data are mostly Minor. Moderate interactions include:
  - Alendronic acid
  - Risedronic acid
  - Ibandronate
  - Tetracycline
  - Baloxavir marboxil

  Minor interactions include several immunosuppressants and corticosteroids (for example azathioprine, dexamethasone, hydrocortisone, adalimumab). Separating doses from chelating or absorption-sensitive drugs such as bisphosphonates and tetracyclines is commonly advised.

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a model score alone (L5). No clinical trials or literature support zinc acetate in severe nonproliferative diabetic retinopathy, and the mechanistic link is speculative. The label-based safety review has not been done.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data for zinc acetate
- SAHPRA Professional Information (warnings, contraindications), and verification that the listed registrations actually contain zinc acetate
- A targeted literature search on zinc and diabetic retinopathy, including zinc status in diabetic patients
- Route and formulation assessment: the registered forms are parenteral, and an oral or other form may be needed for this use
- Review of the porphyria contraindication signal and the interactions listed above
