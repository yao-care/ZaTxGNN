## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Based on general pharmacology, doxylamine is a first-generation H1-receptor antagonist. In allergic urticaria, histamine released from mast cells drives wheals and itching, so blocking H1 receptors is biologically plausible.

The score is very high, but it is a model prediction only. It probably reflects a class effect of antihistamines, so this may be an already-known class use rather than a novel repurposing finding. Doxylamine-specific efficacy and dosing in urticaria are unverified. Sedation and anticholinergic burden are also relevant considerations.

The other predicted indications are weaker or less specific. All are also L5 with a Hold recommendation:

- **Cold urticaria (99.26%)**: a physical urticaria that also involves histamine release, so H1 blockade is plausible. Second-generation antihistamines are usually preferred because they cause less sedation.
- **Nasal cavity disease (99.36%)**: this is a broad category covering allergic rhinitis, sinusitis, polyps and more, so it is too non-specific to support a defined indication.
- **Acute laryngopharyngitis (99.34%)**: this is mostly infectious, and antihistamine benefit is not established. Drying of secretions could plausibly worsen throat discomfort.
