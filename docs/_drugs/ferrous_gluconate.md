# Ferrous Gluconate: From Iron Deficiency Anaemia to Plummer-Vinson Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Ferrous gluconate is an iron supplement, originally used to treat iron deficiency anaemia and related anaemias.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this drug. Based on known information, ferrous gluconate is an iron salt that replenishes the body's iron stores. Its efficacy in iron deficiency anaemia is well established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to Plummer-Vinson syndrome.

Plummer-Vinson syndrome is characterised by iron deficiency anaemia, difficulty swallowing (dysphagia) and oesophageal webs. Iron replacement is the established standard of care and is known to improve both the anaemia and the swallowing symptoms. This makes the prediction mechanistically plausible.

However, this prediction largely overlaps with the drug's existing iron deficiency anaemia indication. The high TxGNN score most likely reflects the close relationship between the two diseases in the knowledge graph, rather than a genuinely novel repurposing signal. No direct trial or literature evidence was found in the data compiled for this report.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindication signals from the knowledge graph** (these are not taken from the product label): peptic ulcer disease, hereditary haemochromatosis, haemosiderosis, iron metabolism disease, Wilson disease, ulcerative colitis, inflammatory bowel disease, haemolytic anaemia and gastroenteritis.

**Drug interactions:** 80 interactions were identified. The main ones from the first 20 reviewed are:

- **Moderate:**
  - Proton pump inhibitors: rabeprazole, omeprazole, lansoprazole, dexlansoprazole, pantoprazole, esomeprazole. Reduced gastric acid may lower iron absorption.
  - Tetracyclines: doxycycline, tetracycline, minocycline.
  - Fluoroquinolone: levofloxacin.
  - Antacids and minerals: magnesium oxide, calcium carbonate, aluminium hydroxide, magaldrate, sodium sulfate.
  - Others: pancrelipase, triethylenetetramine.
- **Minor:** cimetidine, zinc sulfate, zinc acetate.

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for warnings and contraindications. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top prediction, Plummer-Vinson syndrome, is mechanistically plausible but largely duplicates the existing iron deficiency anaemia indication, so it is not a novel repurposing opportunity. No trials or publications support it. The other four predictions have weak or unclear mechanistic links, and the only trial found is a small pilot in a related disorder.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The SAHPRA package insert (PI), to obtain warnings and contraindications and to confirm the approved indication of the registered product. The safety data gap is a blocking item.
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank.
- A targeted literature search on iron replacement in Plummer-Vinson syndrome, to confirm whether the prediction adds anything beyond standard iron deficiency anaemia care.
- A check of whether oral ferrous gluconate formulations are available in South Africa, since the only registration found is an injectable product.
- Review of the knowledge graph contraindication signals (for example inflammatory bowel disease and peptic ulcer disease) against the label before any use in these patients.
