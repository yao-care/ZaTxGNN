## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, ferrous gluconate supplies elemental iron, and its role in correcting iron deficiency is well established.

Plummer-Vinson syndrome combines difficulty swallowing, oesophageal webs and iron-deficiency anaemia. Iron replacement is the accepted mainstay of care. The link between the drug and the disease is therefore biologically plausible, and it probably explains the very high TxGNN score.

This is arguably an on-label-type use of iron rather than true repurposing. The value of this candidate lies in confirming the use locally, not in discovering a new one. The other four predictions for this drug are weaker:

- **Megaloblastic anaemia (B12- and folate-independent), oesophageal malformation and biotin metabolic disease** have no clear iron mechanism. Their scores look like knowledge-graph artefacts.
- **Vitamin deficiency disorder** is indirect, because iron is a mineral, not a vitamin. Four small trials of iron supplementation exist, but none tests correction of a vitamin deficiency.
