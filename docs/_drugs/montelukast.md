## One-Sentence Summary

Montelukast is a leukotriene receptor antagonist that is already marketed in South Africa. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **bronchitis**, with a prediction score of **0.9995**. The data compiled for this report include **17 registered clinical trials** and **20 publications**. The evidence level is **L2**, because small controlled trials in non-asthmatic eosinophilic bronchitis and recurrent obstructive bronchitis exist, but they are small and no large confirmatory trial is documented.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Montelukast is known as a cysteinyl leukotriene receptor antagonist. These leukotrienes are inflammatory mediators that drive airway inflammation, mucus production and cough. A trial of montelukast in RSV bronchiolitis cites raised cysteinyl-leukotriene levels in the airways of infected infants as its rationale.

Mechanistically, blocking this pathway may help in bronchitis, particularly eosinophilic forms. One publication proposes that mast cells and eosinophils contribute to cough hypersensitivity in eosinophilic bronchitis. This gives a plausible link, but it is a hypothesis and not proof of benefit.

Much of the registered trial activity concerns bronchiolitis, a related but distinct infant condition, and transplant-related bronchiolitis obliterans. These studies are only indirect support for bronchitis. Direct evidence comes from the trials in non-asthmatic eosinophilic bronchitis and recurrent obstructive bronchitis.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
- The evidence level is L2. Small controlled trials of montelukast exist in non-asthmatic eosinophilic bronchitis and recurrent obstructive bronchitis, but they are small and their results are not available in the compiled data. Much of the wider trial activity concerns bronchiolitis, which is related but not the same disease.
- Montelukast is already marketed in South Africa, which lowers the barrier to further study.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Full results and quality appraisal of the eosinophilic bronchitis trial (NCT01121016) and the paediatric recurrent bronchitis trial (NCT04613180).
- A larger adequately powered randomised trial in a clearly defined bronchitis population, such as eosinophilic bronchitis.
- Mechanism of action data and the SAHPRA Professional Information warnings and contraindications.
- A paediatric safety and dosing plan, plus a review of the moderate-severity drug interactions listed above.
