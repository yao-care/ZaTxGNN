# Carbamazepine: From Epilepsy to Trigeminal Nerve Neoplasm

## One-Sentence Summary

Carbamazepine is an anticonvulsant originally used for epilepsy, bipolar disorder and trigeminal neuralgia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **trigeminal nerve neoplasm**, but there are **no registered clinical trials** and the **20 publications** retrieved are mostly case reports and reviews of tumour-related neuralgia. They do not show any anti-tumour effect.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Carbamazepine is generally understood to block voltage-gated sodium channels, which reduces abnormal repetitive neuronal firing. This is the basis of its use in epilepsy and trigeminal neuralgia.

The high TxGNN score most likely reflects carbamazepine's established link to **trigeminal neuralgia**, not any effect on tumour biology. The retrieved publications describe neuralgia caused by tumours or masses (schwannoma, meningioma, lipoma, dermoid cyst, pituitary adenoma, lymphoma). In these reports carbamazepine appears only as symptomatic pain treatment, and in several cases it failed to control pain or was poorly tolerated.

At most, carbamazepine could relieve neuralgic pain associated with a tumour. Nothing in the data suggests it treats the neoplasm itself. This is better seen as a symptom-control question than as a true repurposing for neoplasm.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications (from knowledge-graph drug-disease associations, not label text):** The knowledge graph links carbamazepine to a wide range of conditions as contraindications or cautions. These are grouped below.
- **Haematological:** neutropenia, agranulocytosis, pancytopenia, thrombocytopenia, anaemia (including macrocytic), cyclic haematopoiesis, severe congenital neutropenia
- **Cardiac:** atrioventricular block, heart conduction disease, coronary artery disease, sudden cardiac arrest
- **Hepatic and renal:** liver disease, kidney disease, impaired renal function, gallbladder disease
- **Other:** glaucoma, ocular hypertension, hypertension, absence epilepsy, inappropriate ADH syndrome and its nephrogenic form

**Drug Interactions:** The interaction query returned 100 interactions, and 20 are listed in the compiled data. Most are rated Moderate. **Major** interactions include:

Moderate interactions include cimetidine, metronidazole, doxycycline, bupropion, aprepitant, morphine, pioglitazone, canagliflozin, activated charcoal, dronabinol, and several corticosteroids and vitamin D analogues (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone, cholecalciferol, calcifediol, calcitriol, dihydrotachysterol).

Warnings and contraindications from the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) were not available. Please refer to the PI for full safety information. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on carbamazepine's known use in trigeminal neuralgia, not on evidence of activity against tumours. There are no clinical trials, and the literature is limited to case reports and reviews in which carbamazepine was only a symptomatic treatment, sometimes ineffective. The evidence level is L4.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A clear definition of the goal. Symptomatic relief of tumour-associated neuralgia fits within the existing neuralgia indication, whereas anti-tumour use would need preclinical evidence of an effect on tumour biology.
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- The SAHPRA-approved PI, to confirm indications, warnings and contraindications
- Haematological, hepatic and cardiac monitoring plans for the at-risk populations listed above, and a review of the major interactions (clarithromycin, eliglustat)
