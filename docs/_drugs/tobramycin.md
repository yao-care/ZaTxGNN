## One-Sentence Summary

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, tobramycin is an aminoglycoside with broad activity against Gram-negative organisms and *Staphylococcus aureus*, and it is already used topically for bacterial ocular infections.

Exposure keratitis is a corneal surface disorder, typically from incomplete eyelid closure, that can become secondarily infected. The link is therefore plausible as prevention or treatment of secondary bacterial infection, not as treatment of the underlying surface disorder.

The supporting literature is indirect. It consists of bacterial keratitis case reports, susceptibility studies and an in vitro report that aminoglycosides, including tobramycin, are toxic to corneal epithelial cells. That toxicity is a safety concern in an already compromised cornea.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The prediction score is very high (0.9993), but no clinical trials exist and the literature is indirect. Corneal epithelial toxicity is a real concern in a damaged cornea, so the evidence level is L4.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SAHPRA package insert warnings, contraindications and approved indications for Mydex and Tobradex (the safety screening step is currently blocked by this gap)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- A focused evidence review on topical antibiotic prophylaxis or treatment in exposure keratitis, including corneal epithelial safety
- Clarification of the clinical role: preventing secondary infection only, not treating the underlying exposure
- Review of the knowledge-graph contraindications (such as glaucoma and herpetic eye disease) against the approved PI

### Other Predicted Indications (Brief Note)

Other model predictions were reviewed but are not developed further here:
- **Otitis externa** (rank 3): mechanistically strong, since tobramycin covers *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and *S. aureus*. The literature is mostly veterinary or descriptive, plus older case reports in malignant otitis externa, with no controlled trials. Ototoxicity is a concern if the tympanic membrane is not intact. This is a reasonable research question.
- **Post-bacterial disorder** (rank 5): two Phase 3 open-label extension studies (NCT01069705, NCT00982930) of inhaled tobramycin powder in cystic fibrosis. They do not directly match the indication.
- **Infective urethral stricture, epidemic keratoconjunctivitis:** only indirect evidence.
- **Non-human animal disease, postinfectious vasculitis, post-infectious syndrome, Chagas cardiomyopathy, infection-related hemolytic uremic syndrome:** no trials or literature, and no clear mechanistic rationale. In HUS, tobramycin's nephrotoxicity is also a safety concern.
