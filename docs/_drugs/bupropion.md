## Bupropion: From Depression and Nicotine Dependence to Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Bupropion was originally used to treat depressive disorders (including major depressive disorder) and nicotine dependence.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)**,
with **6 registered clinical trials** (including one completed Phase 3 randomised, placebo-controlled trial) and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacological knowledge, bupropion inhibits the reuptake of norepinephrine and dopamine (an NDRI). It has no meaningful serotonergic activity.

ADHD is associated with dopaminergic and noradrenergic dysfunction, and established ADHD treatments (stimulants and atomoxetine) act on these same catecholamine pathways. Bupropion's mechanism therefore plausibly supports improved attention and impulse control. Its original indications (depression and nicotine dependence) also frequently co-occur with ADHD, which helps explain why the model links them.

Reviews in the literature describe bupropion as a second-line or non-stimulant option, mainly for adults who do not respond to or cannot tolerate stimulants. It is not a first-line ADHD treatment.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph** (these come from the TxGNN knowledge graph, not from SAHPRA label text, and must be checked against the Professional Information):
- Neurological and psychiatric: epilepsy, manic bipolar affective disorder, psychotic disorders (including schizophreniform and schizoaffective disorders), anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, substance abuse/dependence (including opiate and morphine dependence), anxiety disorder
- Cardiovascular: hypertension, myocardial infarction
- Hepatic and renal: liver failure, liver disease, cirrhosis, kidney disease, gallbladder disease
- Other: central nervous system neoplasm, hemoglobinopathy

Bupropion is generally known to lower the seizure threshold. This matters for the planned use because many trial populations in ADHD had comorbid substance use disorders.

**Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were identified. Examples of the interactions listed as Major include:
- Opioids: fentanyl, alfentanil, codeine, dihydrocodeine, tramadol
- Stimulants and CNS-active drugs: methylphenidate, amantadine
- Others: lidocaine, hydrocortisone, aldesleukin

Moderate interactions include alprazolam, ethanol, nifedipine, hydrochlorothiazide, cetirizine, chlorpheniramine, acebutolol and alfuzosin. Combination with other ADHD medicines such as methylphenidate needs particular care.

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for warnings and contraindications. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
One completed Phase 3 placebo-controlled trial, a Cochrane review on bupropion in adult ADHD, and several network meta-analyses support this direction, and the NDRI mechanism is plausible. However, evidence consists of small trials, many in comorbid populations. Bupropion is also not first-line for ADHD, and the local registration has no ADHD indication.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The SAHPRA Professional Information (warnings and contraindications), which is a blocking gap for safety screening
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank to confirm the mechanistic link
- Full review of the NCT00048360 and Cochrane results (effect size, dose, tolerability) to confirm the evidence level
- A safety plan covering seizure risk, blood pressure monitoring and interaction screening, especially in patients with substance use comorbidity
- Confirmation of off-label use and EML status in South Africa

**Other predicted indications:** ADHD, inattentive type (L3) has only indirect evidence and should remain a research question. The remaining predictions (faciodigitogenital syndrome, specific developmental disorder, chondromyxoid fibroma, 16p11.2 microdeletion syndrome, hypervitaminosis, benign paroxysmal torticollis of infancy, trigeminal nerve neoplasm, obsolete hypertelorism) have no supporting trials and no plausible mechanism. They should be held.
