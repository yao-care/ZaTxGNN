# Bupropion: From Depression to Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Bupropion is an antidepressant that is also used for smoking cessation, and it is registered in South Africa as an extended-release tablet.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)**,
with **8 clinical trials** and **19 publications** currently supporting this direction, including one completed Phase 3 placebo-controlled trial and a Cochrane review.
Note that the drug is not yet confirmed as the test agent in some of the larger trials.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the source record. From general pharmacology, bupropion is a norepinephrine-dopamine reuptake inhibitor. Unlike most antidepressants, it has no serotonergic activity. It raises dopamine and norepinephrine levels in the brain by blocking their reuptake.

ADHD is linked to reduced dopamine and norepinephrine signalling. Established ADHD drugs act on the same two pathways: stimulants promote catecholamine release, and atomoxetine blocks norepinephrine reuptake. This overlap explains why the model links bupropion to ADHD. Published reviews describe bupropion as a second-line or adjunct option, mainly for patients who do not respond to or cannot tolerate stimulants.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Bupropion has a plausible mechanism, several completed ADHD studies, and a Cochrane review for adults. However, no Phase 3 trial that clearly tests bupropion could be confirmed from the supplied data, so the evidence stays at L2 rather than L1. The product is already marketed in South Africa, but its registered indication text in the data supplied is blank.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Verify at the registry that bupropion was the test agent in NCT00048360 and NCT00061087, and extract their efficacy results. If both are confirmed, the evidence level could be reassessed toward L1.
- Obtain the SAHPRA package insert and extract its warnings and contraindications. This is a blocking gap before safety screening.
- Obtain detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank.
- Set up guardrails: seizure-threshold caution, blood pressure and heart rate monitoring, and caution in patients with substance use or eating disorders.
- Position use as second-line or adjunct, mainly for patients who do not respond to or cannot tolerate stimulants.
- The other nine TxGNN predictions were not developed in this report. Rank 2 (ADHD, inattentive type) has indirect evidence only and is best assessed within the parent ADHD indication. Ranks 3–10 have no plausible mechanistic link or no evidence and should be held.
