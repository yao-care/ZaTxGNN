## Losartan: Malignant hypertensive renal disease
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Losartan is an angiotensin II receptor blocker, originally used to treat hypertension and diabetic nephropathy.
but there are currently **no registered clinical trials** and only **2 publications** (one case report and one animal-model methods paper) supporting this direction. This is largely a model prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, losartan is an AT1 (angiotensin II type 1) receptor blocker. Its efficacy in hypertension is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to renin-angiotensin system (RAS)-driven renovascular hypertension.

Renovascular hypertension is typically driven by reduced renal perfusion, which activates the RAS. Blocking the AT1 receptor is therefore a plausible approach. This is consistent with losartan's approved role in hypertension.

However, the supporting evidence is thin:
- One case report (Takayasu's arteritis) in which losartan was given together with enalapril, so the effect of losartan alone cannot be separated.
- One preclinical methods paper on rat hypertension models.
- No trial in malignant renovascular hypertension.

ARBs also carry a recognised risk of renal function decline in bilateral renal artery stenosis, so any use in this setting would need close monitoring.

**Specific to the predicted indication:** the renal function decline risk of ARBs in bilateral renal artery stenosis or a solitary kidney is highly relevant.

Please refer to the SAHPRA-approved Professional Information (PI) for full warnings and contraindications. Report adverse drug reactions to SAHPRA.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high and the mechanism is plausible, but evidence is limited to one confounded case report and a preclinical methods paper, with no trials. Combined with the renal risk of ARBs in renal artery stenosis, this does not currently justify a repurposing decision.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clinical data (trials or well-designed cohorts) on losartan in renovascular or malignant hypertension
- Mechanism of action data and SAHPRA PI warnings and contraindications (currently missing)
- A renal safety monitoring plan (creatinine, eGFR, potassium) for patients with renal artery stenosis
- Review of how this indication fits against existing standard therapy and the approved hypertension indication
