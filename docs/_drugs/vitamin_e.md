## Vitamin E: Inborn disorder of bilirubin metabolism
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Vitamin E is a fat-soluble vitamin and antioxidant, originally used to correct deficiency in cholestatic liver disease (including benign recurrent and familial intrahepatic cholestasis) and familial isolated vitamin E deficiency.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **inborn disorder of bilirubin metabolism**, but **no clinical trials** are registered and only **1 publication** was retrieved for this indication, and that paper does not test vitamin E as a treatment. This is a model prediction only.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, vitamin E is a fat-soluble antioxidant vitamin, and its use in cholestatic liver disease is to correct the deficiency that arises when bile flow is impaired and fat-soluble vitamins are poorly absorbed.

Inherited defects of bilirubin or bile acid metabolism can cause progressive cholestasis, which is how the model likely links these conditions to vitamin E. The single paper retrieved (PMID 7915305) describes a bile acid synthesis defect (3β-hydroxy-C27-steroid dehydrogenase deficiency) and does not evaluate vitamin E as therapy.

The link is therefore indirect: vitamin E may serve as supportive care for secondary fat-soluble vitamin deficiency, not as a treatment that modifies the underlying disorder.

Other predicted indications were also reviewed. "Bilirubin metabolism disease" (rank 2) has the most literature, but it is mostly observational, review or animal work, with one small 1979 neonatal study (PMID 481976). "Drug-induced liver injury" (rank 4) is supported mainly by animal and in vitro hepatoprotection studies. The remaining predictions have no retrieved evidence or only indirect evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high, but there are no registered trials and the only retrieved paper does not test vitamin E. The plausible role is supportive correction of fat-soluble vitamin deficiency, not disease-specific treatment. The evidence level is L5.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data to support a mechanistic link
- SAHPRA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Clinical studies of vitamin E in inherited bilirubin or bile acid disorders
- Confirmation of which formulations and routes are suitable, as the registered South African products are all parenteral
- If pursuing a related direction, "bilirubin metabolism disease" and "drug-induced liver injury" are better framed as research questions (L4, preclinical or observational support only)
