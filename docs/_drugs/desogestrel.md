## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, desogestrel is a progestin that suppresses gonadotropin secretion and helps with cycle control. Combined with ethinylestradiol, it can supply hormone replacement and protect the endometrium and bone in women with hypothalamic oligo-amenorrhoea.

The link between contraception and amenorrhea is hormonal regulation of the menstrual cycle. Supporting evidence includes a cohort study of bone mineral density in young women with hypothalamic oligoamenorrhoea treated with oral contraceptives (PMID 11725730) and general pharmacodynamic data.

The mechanism is plausible, but in amenorrhea the benefit is mostly cycle management and bone protection, not disease modification. The evidence is also not specific to desogestrel.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high, but the only trial is not desogestrel-specific, and the literature is mostly reviews and contraceptive studies. The PI safety information needed for screening is missing, so the evidence cannot support moving forward yet. Evidence is L3.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- SAHPRA Professional Information (warnings, contraindications and approved indications) for Merdeza and Mercilon
- Mechanism of action data (e.g. from DrugBank)
- Desogestrel-specific trials in hypothalamic or functional amenorrhea, with bone density and cycle outcomes
- Review of the knowledge-graph contraindication signals, especially cardiovascular and metabolic risks, for the target population
